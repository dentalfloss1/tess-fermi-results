GCN Circular 46040: GRB 261024D: AstroSat CZTI detection of a long burst

Event ID: GRB 261024D
Submitter: ABHINAV GOYAL at Indian Institute of Technology Bombay <abhigoyale2003@gmail.com>
Created: 2026-10-26 10:14:30 UTC
Source file: 46040.json

A. Goyal (IITB), A. Arya (IITB), S. Puri (IITB), Harsha K. H. (IUCAA), S. Salunke (IUCAA), G. Waratkar (Caltech/IITB), V. Bhalerao (IITB),  A. Vibhute (IUCAA), D. Bhattacharya (Ashoka University/IUCAA), A. R. Rao (IUCAA/TIFR), and S. Vadawale (PRL) report on behalf of the AstroSat CZTI collaboration:

Analysis of AstroSat CZTI data with the CIFT framework (Sharma et al., 2021, JApA, 42, 73) showed the detection of a GRB 261024D  which was also detected by SVOM/GRM (via notices), and KONUS-Wind (via IPN notices).

The source was detected in the CZT detectors in the 20-200 keV energy range. The light curve peaks at 2026-10-24 21:33:06.85 UTC. The measured peak count rate associated with the burst is 105 (+28, -26) counts/s above the background in the combined data of three quadrants (out of four), with a total of 458 (+195, -196) counts. The local mean background count rate was 211 (+2, -2) counts/s. We measure a T90 of 11 (+7, -5) s from the cumulative CZT light curve. We caution that there is a 0.3 s dead time in the CZTI data just in the burst, hence the actual T90 may be slightly longer. 

The source was also detected in the CsI anticoincidence (Veto) detector in the 100-500 keV energy range. The light curve showed a peak at 2026-10-24 21:33:06.86 UTC. The measured peak count rate associated with the burst is 405 (+49, -51) counts/s above the background in the combined data of three quadrants (out of four), with a total of 1698 (+303, -328) counts. The local mean background count rate was 520 (+3 -4) counts/s. We measure a T90 of 13 (+10, -5) s from the cumulative Veto light curve.

CZTI data products like interactive and downloadable light curves for this GRB can be found at: 
https://astrosat.iucaa.in/cift/cift_products/530573567.36/S530573567.36_details.html

CZTI is built by a TIFR-led consortium of institutes across India, including VSSC, URSC, IUCAA, SAC, and PRL. The Indian Space Research Organisation funded, managed, and facilitated the project.

CZTI GRB detections are reported regularly on the payload site at:
http://astrosat.iucaa.in/czti/?q=grb

GCN Circular 46051: GRB 261024D: NuSTAR detection of prompt emission

Event ID: GRB 261024D
Submitter: Gaurav Waratkar at Caltech <gauravwaratkar@iitb.ac.in>
Created: 2026-10-26 21:58:49 UTC
Source file: 46051.json

G. Waratkar (Caltech) and B. Grefenstette (Caltech) report on behalf of the NuSTAR Search for INteresting Gamma-ray Signals (SINGS) working group:

The NuSTAR SINGS working group reports the detection of prompt emission from the long-duration GRB 261024D in both the NuSTAR CsI anti-coincidence shields. This GRB was identified through a blind search using the CsI shield rates. Details of the search algorithm will be described in a future paper.

The NuSTAR SINGS algorithm triggered at 2026-10-24T21:33:05.000 UTC shows a detection of GRB 261024D, also detected by SVOM-GRM (GCN Notices), KONUS-Wind (GCN Notices), and AstroSat-CZTI (GCN 46040).

The NuSTAR CsI shield data are recorded at 1 Hz. We detect a single-peaked burst lasting for about 10-s. The peak count rate is ~1500-cps with a baseline rate of ~1000-cps during this time period. We do not see any evidence in the signal above 100 keV in the CZT detectors.

The SVOM-GRM localization at RA = 209.99, Dec = -47.33 implies an offset from the NuSTAR boresight of 100-deg and an offset from the geocenter of 102-deg.

Lightcurves and analysis for this GRB can be found here:
https://nustarsoc.caltech.edu/NuSTAR_Public/grbs/reports/2026/261024D 

Information on NuSTAR SINGS can be found here: 
https://nustarsoc.caltech.edu/NuSTAR_Public/grbs/     

NuSTAR is a NASA Small Explorer mission led by Caltech and managed by JPL for NASA's Science Mission Directorate in Washington.

GCN Circular 46053: GRB 261024D: Glowbug gamma-ray detection

Event ID: GRB 261024D
Submitter: C.C. Cheung at Naval Research Lab <Teddy.Cheung@nrl.navy.mil>
Created: 2026-10-27 01:24:21 UTC
Source file: 46053.json

C.C. Cheung, R. Woolf, M. Kerr, J.E. Grove (NRL), A. Goldstein (USRA), C.A. Wilson-Hodge, D. Kocevski (MSFC), and M.S. Briggs (UAH) report:

The Glowbug gamma-ray telescope [1,2,3], operating on the International Space Station, reports the detection of GRB 261024D, which was also detected by AstroSat (GCN 46040), NuSTAR (GCN 46051), SVOM (sb26102402), and Konus-Wind (Trig_Time 21:33:02.529).  

Using an adaptive window with a resolution of 32-ms, the burst onset is determined to be 2026-10-24 21:33:03.736 with a duration of 9.2 s and a total significance of about 43.1 sigma. The light curve comprises a triple-peaked structure.

The analysis results presented here are preliminary and use a response function that lacks a detailed characterization of the surrounding passive structure of the ISS. Glowbug is a NASA-funded technology demonstrator for sensitive, low-cost gamma-ray transient telescopes developed, built, and operated by the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory (NRL) with support from the University of Alabama in Huntsville, USRA, and NASA MSFC.  

[1] Grove, J.E. et al. 2020, Proc. Yamada Conf. LXXI, arXiv:2009.11959 
[2] Woolf, R.S. et al. 2022, Proc. SPIE, 12181, id. 121811O 
[3] Woolf, R.S. et al. 2024, Proc. SPIE, 13151, id. 1315108

GCN Circular 46061: GRB 261024D: GECAM-B observation

Event ID: GRB 261024D
Submitter: Xinghao Luo at IHEP <2952704891@qq.com>
Created: 2026-10-27 14:59:59 UTC
Source file: 46061.json

Xing-Hao Luo, Chen-Wei Wang, Ren-Yang Zhao, Shao-Lin Xiong (IHEP) report on behalf of GECAM team:

GECAM-B was triggered on-ground by GRB 261024D, at 2026-10-24T21:33:05.250 UTC (denoted as T0), which is also detected by AstroSat CZTI (A. Goyal.et., GCN#46040), NuSTAR (G. Waratkar.et., GCN#46051) and Glowbug gamma-ray telescope (C. C. Cheung.et.al., GCN#46053).

According to the GECAM-B light curves in about 70-6000 keV, this burst mainly consists of multiple pulses with a duration (T90) of 9.5 +1.0/-0.5 s.

The on-ground calculated location, using the GECAM-B trigger data, is RA = 208.64, Dec = 25.1, with a statistical uncertainty of 5.46 degrees.

The GECAM-B light curve can be found here:
https://www.bursthub.cn//gcn/2026/gecambgrb261024D.png

Gravitational wave high-energy Electromagnetic Counterpart All-sky Monitor (GECAM) mission originally consists of two micro-satellites (GECAM-A and GECAM-B) launched in Dec. 2020. As the third member of GECAM constellation, GECAM-C was launched onboard SATech-01 experimental satellite in July 2022. GECAM mission is funded by the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS).

GCN Circular 46065: GRB 261024D: Insight-HXMT/HE detection

Event ID: GRB 261024D
Submitter: Xinghao Luo at IHEP <2952704891@qq.com>
Created: 2026-10-28 04:41:34 UTC
Source file: 46065.json

Xing-Hao Luo, Chen-Wei Wang, Chao Zheng, and Shao-Lin Xiong (IHEP) report on behalf of the Insight-HXMT team:

At 2026-10-24T21:33:06.850 (T0), Insight-HXMT/HE detected the GRB 261024D, which is also detected by AstroSat CZTI (A. Goyal et.al., GCN#46040), NuSTAR (G. Waratkar et.al, GCN#46051), Glowbug gamma-ray telescope (C. C. Cheung et.al., GCN#46053), and GECAM-B (Xing-Hao Luo et.al., GCN#46061).
	
The Insight-HXMT/HE light curve mainly consists of multiple pulses with a T90 of 9.0 +1.5/-0.5 s. The 1s peak rate, measured from T0+0.25 s, is 2957 cnts/sec. Insight-HXMT/HE detected a total of 11613 counts from this burst.

The HXMT/HE light curve can be found here:
https://www.bursthub.cn/gcn/2026/hxmtgrb261024D.png

All measurements above are made with the CsI detectors of Insight-HXMT/HE operating in the regular mode with the energy range of about 60-900 keV (deposited energy). Only gamma-rays with energy greater than about 200 keV can penetrate the spacecraft and leave signals in the CsI detectors installed inside of the telescope.

Insight-HXMT is the first Chinese space X-ray telescope, which was funded jointly by the China National Space Administration (CNSA) and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS). More information about it could be found at: http://hxmten.ihep.ac.cn/
