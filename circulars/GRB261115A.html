The Fermi GBM team reports the detection of a likely LONG GRB

"At 22:10:53.44 UT on 15 November 2026, the Fermi Gamma-ray Burst Monitor (GBM)
triggered and located GRB 261115A (trigger 816473458/261115924).

The on-ground calculated location, using the Fermi GBM trigger data,
is RA = 5.9, Dec = -31.1 (J2000 degrees, equivalent to J2000 0h 23m 26.4s, -31d 5'),
with a statistical uncertainty of 2.13 degrees.

The angle from the Fermi LAT boresight is 37 degrees.

GCN Circular 46305: GRB 261115A: GECAM-B observation

Event ID: GRB 261115A
Submitter: Jin-Ye Luo <jy_luo2002@163.com>
Created: 2026-11-16 14:39:08 UTC
Source file: 46305.json

Jin-Ye Luo, Chen-Wei Wang, Yue Wang, Zheng-Hang Yu, Shao-Lin Xiong (IHEP) report on behalf of GECAM team: 

GECAM-B was triggered in-flight by GRB 261115A at 2026-11-15T22:10:54.000 UTC (denoted as T0), which is also detected by Fermi/GBM (Fermi GBM team, GCN #46299).

According to the GECAM-B light curves in about 70-6000 keV, this burst mainly consists of multiple pulses with a duration (T90) of 4.0 +3.5/-0.5 s.

The on-ground calculated location, using the GECAM-B trigger data, is RA = 3.3 deg, Dec = -26.3 deg, with a statistical uncertainty of 5.0 degrees.

With this localization, the time-averaged spectrum from T0-1.25 to T0+3.25 s is best fitted by a power law function. The power law index is -2.23 +0.17/-0.15. The event fluence (10-1000 keV) in this time interval is (6.8 +1.1/−0.8)E-06 erg/cm^2. 

The GECAM-B light curve can be found here:
https://www.bursthub.cn/gcn/2026/gecambgrb261115A.png

Gravitational wave high-energy Electromagnetic Counterpart All-sky Monitor (GECAM) mission originally consists of two micro-satellites (GECAM-A and GECAM-B) launched in Dec. 2020. As the third member of GECAM constellation, GECAM-C was launched onboard SATech-01 experimental satellite in July 2022. GECAM mission is funded by the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS).
