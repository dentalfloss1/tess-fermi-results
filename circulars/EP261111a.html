GCN Circular 46226: EP261111a: Einstein Probe detection of an X-ray transient

Event ID: EP261111a
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-11 14:03:15 UTC
Source file: 46226.json

G. L. Huang, Z. X. Li (IHEP, CAS), Y. J. Zhang (THU), W. D. Zhang (NAO, CAS) on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:

We report on the detection of an X-ray transient by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission, designated EP261111a. The transient triggered EP-WXT (ID: 01709322401) at 2026-11-11T13:25:10 (UTC). The WXT position of the source is R.A. = 43.118 deg, DEC = 44.444 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 3 arcmin in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic).

A follow-up observation with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) was performed automatically. Within the WXT error circle, an uncatalogued X-ray source was detected at R.A. = 43.1202 deg, DEC = 44.4365 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 20 arcsec in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic).

Further information will be updated when the telemetry data is received.

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

GCN Circular 46235: EP261111a: Refined analysis of EP-FXT observations

Event ID: EP261111a
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-12 09:58:53 UTC
Source file: 46235.json

Z.X. Li, G. L. Huang (IHEP, CAS), Y. J. Zhang (THU), W. D. Zhang (NAO, CAS) on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:

The fast X-ray transient EP261111a triggered the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission at 2026-11-11T13:25:10 (UTC) (Huang et al., GCN 46226). Subsequent optical follow-up observations were reported by Jin et al. (GCN 46227) and Buckley et al. (GCN 46230). 

The refined analysis of the WXT data shows that the event started at T0 = 2026-11-11T13:24:09 (UTC), and lasted for approximately 60 seconds, after which the WXT light curve was interrupted due to the autonomous follow-up observation. During the flare, the average WXT 0.5-4 keV spectrum can be fitted with an absorbed power law with a fixed Galactic hydrogen column density (NH) of 1.10×10^21 cm^-2, and a photon index of 1.23 (-0.64/+0.67). The derived average unabsorbed 0.5-4 keV flux is 1.30 (-0.43/+0.65) ×10^(-9) erg/s/cm^2. 
 
The Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) on board EP observed this source autonomously at 2026-11-11T13:28:25 (UTC, T0+256s). The exposure time of this observation is 3150 s. On-ground analysis of the FXT data found an uncatalogued source at R.A. = 43.1193,  DEC = 44.4351 (J2000) with an uncertainty of about 10 arcsec (radius, 90% C.L. statistical and systematic) within the WXT error circle. The average 0.5-10 keV spectrum can be fitted with an absorbed power law with a fixed Galactic hydrogen column density of 1.10×10^21 cm^-2 and a photon index of 1.90 (-0.14/+0.15). The derived average unabsorbed 0.5-10 keV flux is 3.15 (-0.36/+0.41)×10^(-12) erg/s/cm^2. All uncertainties are reported at the 90% confidence interval.

Further FXT follow-up observations have been arranged.

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

GCN Circular 46236: EP261111a: LCO optical upper limits

Event ID: EP261111a
Submitter: Rob Eyles-Ferris at U of Leicester <raje1@leicester.ac.uk>
Created: 2026-11-12 11:41:14 UTC
Source file: 46236.json

R. A. J. Eyles-Ferris (Leicester), J. Chácon (PUC), P. G. Jonker (Radboud), D. B. Malesani (DAWN/NBI and Radboud) and A. A. Chrimes (Radboud) report on behalf of a larger collaboration:

We observed the field of the fast X-ray transient EP261111a detected by Einstein Probe (Huang et al., GCN 46226; Li et al., GCN 46235) with LCO 1-m telescopes located at McDonald Observatory (USA) and equipped with SINISTRO instruments. Observations consisted of a sequence of 6 x 300 s exposures in each of the SDSS r and z bands starting at 2026-11-12 01:59:08 UT (~12.4 h post trigger) and 2026-11-12 08:12:07 UT (~18.8 h post trigger), respectively.

We performed image subtraction using PanSTARRS template images and compared the stacked and templates manually. We do not detect any new source inside the Einstein Probe Follow-up X-ray Telescope uncertainty region (Li et al., GCN 46235) to 3-sigma limiting magnitudes of r > 22.7 and z > 22.3 in either our stacked images or the subtractions. Our non-detections are consistent with those of Jin et al. (GCN 46227) and Buckley et al. (GCN 46230).

Our upper limits are in AB magnitudes, calibrated using nearby stars from the PanSTARRS catalogue, and are not corrected for Galactic extinction.

GCN Circular 46237: EP261111a: K and J band upper limits by SYSU 80cm infrared telescope

Event ID: EP261111a
Submitter: linp@mail2.sysu.edu.cn
Created: 2026-11-12 11:43:24 UTC
Source file: 46237.json

Pu Lin, Jin-Ji Li, Zhi-Xiang Tan, Zhong-Nan Dong, Yun Shi, Rui-Chen Gao, Jia-Qi Lin, Hao-Nan Yang, Hao-Ran Zhang, Jian-Hong Cao, Shu-Quan Bai, Si-Rui Huang, Jing-Kai Sun, Lu-Yao Wen, P. H. Thomas Tam, Rong-Feng Shen, and Bin Ma (Sun Yat-sen University) report on behalf of the SYSU 80cm infrared telescope team: 

We observed the field of EP261111a detected by the Einstein Probe (G. L. Huang et al., GCN 46226 and Z. X. Li et al., GCN 46235) with the Sun Yat-sen University 80cm infrared telescope in the K and J bands. The observations were carried out in the K band on 2026-11-11 from 15:02:09 to 15:41:58 UT (1.62 - 2.28 hr after the EP/WXT trigger) using stacked images (113 x 20s, 2260 s total exposure), and in the J band on 2026-11-11 from 16:40:41 to 17:41:30 UT (3.26 - 4.27 hr after the EP/WXT trigger) using stacked images (172 x 20s, 3440 s total exposure).

We do not detect any new source within the localization (RA = 43.1193 deg, Dec = +44.4351 deg, J2000; 10 arcsec uncertainty; reported by Einstein Probe, Z. X. Li et al., GCN 46235) in our stacked images, and obtain 5-sigma upper limits of K = 17.83 mag (Vega) and J = 19.83 mag (Vega).

The photometry was calibrated against nearby stars from the 2MASS catalog and is reported in the Vega system, without correction for Galactic extinction.

The SYSU 80cm infrared telescope is operated and managed by the Department of Astronomy, Sun Yat-sen University.
