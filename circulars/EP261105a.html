GCN Circular 46155: EP261105a: Einstein Probe detection of an X-ray transient

Event ID: EP261105a
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-05 12:13:57 UTC
Source file: 46155.json

C.L. Guo,  B.B. Zhang(NAO, CAS), Y. Q. Zhao(PRIC, USTC), Q. Y. Wu, W. D. Zhang(NAO, CAS) on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:
 
We report on the detection of an X-ray transient by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission, designated EP261105a. The transient triggered EP-WXT (ID: 01709322270) at 2026-11-05T10:07:54(UTC). The WXT position of the source is R.A. = 91.013 deg, DEC = -54.231 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 3 arcmin in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic). 
 
A follow-up observation with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) was performed automatically. Within the WXT error circle, an uncatalogued X-ray source was detected at R.A. = 91.0035 deg, DEC = -54.2664 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 20 arcsec in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic).

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

GCN Circular 46160: EP261105a: SVOM/VT optical candidate

Event ID: EP261105a
Submitter: Yinuo Ma <mayn@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-05 15:11:02 UTC
Source file: 46160.json

Y. N. Ma, H. L. Li, L. P. Xin, Y. L. Qiu, C. Wu, Z. H. Yao, J. R. Xu, X. H. Han, Y. Xu, J. Wang, P. P. Zhang, W. J. Xie, Y. J. Xiao, H. B. Cai, J. S. Deng, J. Y. Wei (NAOC), J. Palmerio (CEA) report on behalf of the SVOM/VT team. 

SVOM/VT performed a Target of Opportunity observation of EP261105a detected by EP/WXT (Guo et al., GCN 46155). SVOM/VT began observing the field at 2026-11-05T10:51:11 UTC, 0.72 hours after the trigger, in the VT_B (400nm-650nm) and VT_R (650nm-1000nm) channels simultaneously. 

With X-band data available, an uncatalogued candidate (compared to the Legacy Survey), within the 20 arcsec EP/FXT error circle (Guo et al., GCN 46155), is detected in our stacked VT_R image at coordinates R.A., Dec. 91.00143, -54.26428 degrees, corresponding to:

R.A. (J2000)= 06:04:00.34
Dec. (J2000)= -54:15:51.38

with an uncertainty of ~0.5 arcsec.

The preliminary photometric measurements in AB magnitudes are as follows:

mid time (h) | exposure time (s) | band |  mag (AB) 
-------------|-------------------|------|-----------------
     0.87    |      20*50        | VT_B |  >23.1 (3-sigma)
     0.85    |      18*50        | VT_R |  22.3+/-0.2 

Our photometry was not corrected for Galactic extinction. More detailed investigation will be performed once more data are available.

The Space Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA, China), National Center for Space Studies (CNES, France) and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS, China), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. VT was jointly developed by Xi'an Institute of Optics and Precision Mechanics (XIOPM), CAS and National astronomical observatories (NAOC), CAS.

GCN Circular 46162: EP261105a: LCO optical upper limits

Event ID: EP261105a
Submitter: J. Sánchez-Sierras at Radboud University <javisansie@gmail.com>
Created: 2026-11-05 17:07:33 UTC
Source file: 46162.json

A. P. C. van Hoof (Radboud), G. Corcoran (UCD), J. Sanchez-Sierras (Radboud), A. Martin-Carrillo (UCD), P. G. Jonker (Radboud) report on behalf of a larger collaboration:

We observed the field of the fast X-ray transient EP261105a detected by Einstein Probe (Guo et al., GCN 46155) with the LCO 1-m telescope located at Siding Spring (Australia) and equipped with the SINISTRO instrument. Observations consisted of a sequence of 9 x 300 s exposures in SDSS r-band and started at 2026-11-05 at 12:41:00 UT (~2.55 h after the trigger).

We performed image subtraction against archival Legacy Survey and Skymapper images. We do not detect any new source inside the Einstein Probe Follow-up X-ray Telescope uncertainty region, including the counterpart candidate detected by Ma et al. (GCN 46160), down to a 3-sigma upper limiting magnitude of:

r > 23.0

This upper limit is in AB magnitude, calibrated using nearby stars from the Legacy Survey catalogue, and is not corrected for Galactic extinction.
