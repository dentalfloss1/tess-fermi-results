GCN Circular 46155: EP261105a: Einstein Probe detection of an X-ray transient

Event ID: EP261105a
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-05 12:13:57 UTC

C.L. Guo,  B.B. Zhang(NAO, CAS), Y. Q. Zhao(PRIC, USTC), Q. Y. Wu, W. D. Zhang(NAO, CAS) on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:
 
We report on the detection of an X-ray transient by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission, designated EP261105a. The transient triggered EP-WXT (ID: 01709322270) at 2026-11-05T10:07:54(UTC). The WXT position of the source is R.A. = 91.013 deg, DEC = -54.231 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 3 arcmin in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic). 
 
A follow-up observation with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) was performed automatically. Within the WXT error circle, an uncatalogued X-ray source was detected at R.A. = 91.0035 deg, DEC = -54.2664 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 20 arcsec in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic).

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

We observed the field of the fast X-ray transient EP261105a detected by Einstein Probe (Guo et al., GCN 46155) with the LCO 1-m telescope located at Siding Spring (Australia) and equipped with the SINISTRO instrument. GCN Circular 46168: EP261105a: refined analysis of the EP-WXT and follow-up EP-FXT observations

Event ID: EP261105a
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-06 08:33:37 UTC
Source file: 46168.json

C. L. Guo, B. B. Zhang, Q.Y. Wu, W.D. Zhang (NAO, CAS), Y. Q. Zhao(PRIC, USTC)
on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team: 

The fast X-ray transient EP261105a was detected by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission (Guo et al., GCN 46155), and followed by several telescopes (Ma et al. GCN 46160, van Hoof et al. GCN 46162, Buckley et al. GCN 46165). The WXT observation started at T0=2026-11-05 10:03:24 (UTC), and lasted for around 270 s before being interrupted by autonomous follow-up. The WXT light curve shows an increase in flux from about T0+180 s, and starts to decreas from about T0+200 s. The average WXT 0.5–4 keV spectrum can be fitted with an absorbed power-law model with a fixed Galactic hydrogen column density of 4.84 × 10^20 cm^−2 and a photon index of 0.92 (+0.83/−0.77). The derived average unabsorbed 0.5–4 keV flux is 2.58 (+1.75/−1.14) × 10^−10 erg s^−1 cm^−2. 

The autonomous follow-up observation with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) on board EP started at 2026-11-05 10:10:39 (UTC), 435 s after T0. The exposure time was 4354 s. The on-ground analysis of the FXT data detected an uncatalogued X-ray source at R.A. = 91.0018 deg, DEC = -54.2637 deg (J2000), with an uncertainty of 10 arcsec in radius (90% C.L., including statistical and systematic uncertainties). The average FXT 0.5–10 keV spectrum can be fitted with an absorbed power-law model with a fixed Galactic hydrogen column density of 4.84 × 10^20 cm^−2 and a photon index of 2.11 (+0.11/−0.11). The derived average unabsorbed 0.5–10 keV flux is 4.12 (+0.30/−0.28) × 10^−12 erg s^−1 cm^−2. The uncertainties quoted above are at the 90% confidence level.

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory designed to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

GCN Circular 46170: EP261105a: VLT/FORS2 optical detection

Event ID: EP261105a
Submitter: Roberto Ricci at INAF-IRA <ricci@ira.inaf.it>
Created: 2026-11-06 11:52:47 UTC
Source file: 46170.json

Hira Waseem, Muskan Yadav, Nicolas Monsalves, Narges Shahamat, Roberto Ricci, Niccolò Passaleva, Yuhan Yang, and Eleonora Troja (U Rome) report on behalf of the ERC BHianca team:

We observed the field of EP261105a detected by Einstein Probe (Guo et al., GCN 46155; Guo et al. 46168) with the FORS2 imager on the ESO VLT UT1 (Antu). FORS2 observations began on 2026-11-06 at 04:10 UT, 18.0 hours after  the trigger and were carried out in the R-band at an average airmass ~1.5 and with an average seeing ~0.6 arcsec.

At the position of the candidate optical afterglow reported by SVOM/VT (Ma et al., GCN 46160) we detect a source with R ~ 24.3 AB mag, consistent with the non-detection reported by LCO at 2.6 hours (van Hoof et al., GCN 46162). Compared with the SVOM/VT detection (VT_R = 22.3 at 0.85 hours; Ma et al., GCN 46160), our measurement implies a power-law decay with temporal index alpha ~ -0.5. Magnitudes are calibrated  using nearby stars in the Legacy Survey DR10 catalog (Dey et al. 2019), and are not corrected for Galactic extinction.

No catalogued source is visible at the position of the optical counterpart, implying that any underlying galaxy would be faint. We note that the counterpart is located ~4 arcsec from the centre of a bright (r~18 AB mag)  galaxy with photometric redshift of z = 0.055 +/- 0.014 in the Legacy Survey DR10. The chance coincidence probability for this galaxy is P_cc ~ 0.01  (Bloom et al. 2002) . If the association is confirmed, the projected offset corresponds to ~4.5 kpc at z = 0.055. 

Further observations are planned.

We thank the staff at the VLT for the rapid execution of these observations.

GCN Circular 46191: EP261105a: VLT/HAWK-I nIR detection

Event ID: EP261105a

Created: 2026-11-08 06:57:43 UTC
Source file: 46191.json

H. Waseem, M. El Kabir, M. Yadav, R. Ricci  and E. Troja (U Rome) report on behalf of the ERC BHianca team:

We observed the field of EP261105a (Guo et al., GCN 46155; Guo et al., GCN 46168) with HAWK-I on the ESO Very Large Telescope (VLT). Observations started on 2026-11-07 at 08:12 UT, about 46 hours after the trigger, and were performed using the J and Ks filters at an airmass of ~1.2 and an average seeing of ~0.5 arcsec. 

Photometric calibration was performed using nearby stars from the 2MASS catalogue. Magnitudes are reported in the AB system and are not corrected for Galactic extinction. At the position of the optical counterpart (Ma et al., GCN 46160; Waseem et al., GCN 46170), we detect the source at a preliminary magnitude of J~24 AB. 

If associated with the nearby galaxy at z_phot~0.055, the observed magnitudes would be much fainter than AT2017gfo at a similar epoch. Our observations continue to be consistent with a fading non-thermal afterglow, and the presence of an underlying galaxy cannot be yet constrained. 

We thank the staff at the VLT for the rapid execution of these observations.

GCN Circular 46204: EP261105a: Second EP-FXT follow-up observation

Event ID: EP261105a
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-09 09:48:38 UTC
Source file: 46204.json

C. L. Guo, B. B. Zhang, Q.Y. Wu, W.D. Zhang (NAO, CAS), Y. Q. Zhao(PRIC, USTC)
on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team: 

The fast X-ray transient EP261105a was detected by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission (Guo et al., GCN 46155). A refined analysis of the EP-WXT and autonomous EP-FXT observations was subsequently reported by Guo et al. (GCN 46168). Optical and near-infrared follow-up observations were reported by Ma et al. (GCN 46160), van Hoof et al. (GCN 46162), Buckley et al. (GCN 46165), Waseem et al. (GCN 46170), Passaleva et al. (GCN 46172), and Waseem et al. (GCN 46191). 

The EP-FXT performed a second follow-up observation of EP261105a beginning at 2026-11-06 03:27:37 (UTC), with an exposure of about 5363 s. The on-ground analysis of the FXT data detected an uncatalogued X-ray source at R.A. = 91.0002 deg, DEC = -54.2620 deg (J2000), with an uncertainty of 10 arcsec in radius (90% C.L., including statistical and systematic uncertainties), consistent with the X-ray source reported before by Guo et al. (GCN 46168). The average FXT 0.5–10 keV spectrum can be fitted with an absorbed power-law model with a fixed Galactic hydrogen column density of 4.84 × 10^20 cm^−2 and a photon index of 2.05(+1.79/-1.11). The derived average unabsorbed 0.5–10 keV flux is 5.31 (+6.48/-2.75) × 10^−14 erg s^−1 cm^−2. The uncertainties quoted above are at the 90% confidence level.

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory designed to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

GCN Circular 46209: EP261105A: Gemini-South/GMOS Optical Upper Limit

G. Srinivasaragavan (Northwestern) and J. Rastinejad (UMD) report on behalf of a larger collaboration:

We observed the field of EP261105A detected by Einstein Probe (Guo et al., GCN 46155; Guo et al., GCN 46168) with the GMOS instrument on Gemini South (PI: Srinivasaragavan). GMOS observations began on 2026-11-07 at 07:27:15 UT, ~1.9 days after the WXT trigger in r’-band.

The images were calibrated to Legacy Survey DR11 (Dey et al. 2019). We do not detect any source at the position of the reported optical counterpart (Ma et al., GCN 46160; Waseem et al., GCN 46170, GCN 46191) to a 3-sigma limiting magnitude of r > 24.6 AB mag, not corrected for Galactic extinction. If associated with the reported nearby galaxy at z = 0.055 (Waseem et al., GCN 46170), this corresponds to an absolute magnitude upper limit of r > -12.4 AB mag, much fainter than kilonovae similar to AT2017gfo at a similar epoch. 

We thank the staff at Gemini observatory for the rapid scheduling and execution of these observations.
