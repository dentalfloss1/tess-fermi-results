GRB 260903A: SVOM detection of a soft faint burst 

M. Brunet, O. Godet, H. Yang (IRAP), R.-C. Chen (NJU), T. Xiao (GXU) report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:

At 2026-09-03T12:37:20 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 260903A (SVOM burst-id sb26090302).

The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was detected both by the Count-Rate Trigger (CRT) and the Image Trigger (IMT), which produced a sequence of 2 alerts. CRT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 7.70 in the [5-8] keV energy band over a time window of 20.40 seconds starting at 2026-09-03T12:36:59 UTC.

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 25.614, 11.389 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 1h42m27.28s
Dec. (J2000) = 11d23m21.52s
 with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 10.20 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

The SVOM/ECLAIRs light curve showed a broad structure with a duration of about 20 s.

Due to the detection significance being below the slew threshold, no immediate slew was performed on this burst.
No X-ray observation could be performed by SVOM/MXT for the time being.
No optical observation could be performed by SVOM/VT for the time being.
A SVOM ToO has been scheduled.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. SVOM/ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC. SVOM/GRM was developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS. SVOM/MXT was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IJCLab, University of Leicester, MPE.

The Burst Advocate (BA) on shift for this alert is R.-C. Chen: chrczxx@smail.nju.edu.cn.
Please contact the BA by email if you require additional information.

r = 20.46 +/- 0.19 AB (mid-time 2.30 hr after the trigger);
r = 21.8 +/- 0.4 AB (mid-time 2.74 hr after the trigger).

Overall, there is no strong evidence for variability, though the source is only barely detected in our last epoch, suggesting some fading compared to our earlier epochs and to the SVOM/VT measurement (Li et al., GCN 45504).

D. B. Malesani (DAWN/NBI), J. An (NAOC), G. Corcoran (UCD), A. Saccardi (CEA/Irfu), F. Mustafaj (NOT), O. Stalder (NOT), report on behalf of a larger collaboration:

We observed the optical counterpart (Li et al., GCN 45504; Saccardi et al., GCN 45505) of the SVOM/ECLAIRs GRB 260903A (Brunet et al., GCN 45499) using the Nordic Optical Telescope (NOT) equipped with the ALFOSC camera. A total of 3x300 s observations were secured in the SDSS r band.

The optical afterglow is detected in our images. Preliminary photometry against the Pan-STARRS catalog yields a magnitude r = 22.88 +- 0.16 (AB) on 2026 Sep 4.00 UT, that is 11.45 hr after the trigger.

Our measurement indicates significant fading compared to previously reported measurements (Li et al., GCN 45504; Saccardi et al., GCN 45505), thus confirming that this object is the afterglow of GRB 260903A.

GCN Circular 45517: GRB 260903A: SVOM/ECLAIRs refined analysis

Event ID: GRB 260903A
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-09-04 17:41:50 UTC
Source file: 45517.json

M. Brunet, H. Yang, O. Godet (IRAP) , W.-J. Xie (NAOC), R.-C. Chen (NJU), T. Xiao (GXU), report on behalf of the SVOM/ECLAIRs team:

Using the event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station, we report further analysis of SVOM/ECLAIRs observations of GRB 260903A (SVOM burst-id sb26090302 – GCN 45499, trigger time T0 = 2026-09-03T12:37:20 UTC).

The burst that triggered ECLAIRs onboard shows a single peak lightcurve. The burst duration is T90 = 21 +6/-4 s in the 4-20 keV energy band.

The time-averaged spectrum from T0-18.98 s to T0+1.13 s in the 5-120 keV energy range is best fitted by a powerlaw model, with alpha = -3.2/-0.5. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is (1.6 +0.3/-0.2)e-7 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is 0.70 +/-0.08 ph/cm^2/s. 

A blackbody model also gives a good fit with kT = 1.8 +/-0.2 keV. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is (1.2 +/-0.2)e-7 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is 0.54 +/-0.06 ph/cm^2/s. 

The spectral parameters show that this burst could be classified as an X-Ray Flash (XRF).

All the quoted errors are at the 68% confidence level.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC.

The SVOM/ECLAIRs point of contact for this burst is: Marius Brunet (IRAP) (marius.brunet et utoulouse.fr)

We observed the GRB 260903A in the gamma ray central detector of the SNAPPY CubeSat. This is the first Gamma Ray Burst seen by SNAPPY operating a GAGG:Ce ultra fast detector, with an active charged particle veto in polar orbit launched on May 4, 2026 UTC and began stable data taking August 4, 2026. 

The Cubesat detector energy spectrum (https://soar.wichita.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/556ce006-a865-490c-bb15-90a62057e438/content?authentication-token=eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9.eyJlaWQiOiJhOWVmMDM5OC02OTcxLTQyN2QtYTI3Mi01ZDU3YjliMWE0NTQiLCJzZyI6W10sImF1dGhlbnRpY2F0aW9uTWV0aG9kIjoicGFzc3dvcmQiLCJleHAiOjE3OTI1MTc1OTB9.YMDaDxRv7TOpt-DurQ8eeccaqLfmXCV69LTOrmpCt2s) shows 11,473 gamma rays that were only those events in the gamma ray detection without any associated charged particle signal in the veto array. It is an ultra quiet detector being highly shielding by 1 cm equivalent of steel resulting in a 50 keV lower edge cut off which results in low count rates during normal operations. 

The timing graph (https://soar.wichita.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/a6567332-ee57-4125-acb2-54eadfd4a605/content?authentication-token=eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9.eyJlaWQiOiJhOWVmMDM5OC02OTcxLTQyN2QtYTI3Mi01ZDU3YjliMWE0NTQiLCJzZyI6W10sImF1dGhlbnRpY2F0aW9uTWV0aG9kIjoicGFzc3dvcmQiLCJleHAiOjE3OTI1MTcxNjN9.8cYeYUFCF51IygC0cHEg6OeimY_giGXILXJpIYuJhdI) for the gamma ray burst was about 80 seconds long with 11,457 events in the main peak, but a small number of events, 16, before the burst can be seen distributed over 400 seconds, we believe these to be associated with the burst. The time was Sept. 3, 2026 at 12:26:25 UTC, but the CubeSat clock in UNIX time is set by ground station uploads via S-band and can be off from GPS time. The CubeSat has no directionality information but the spacecraft at the time of the burst was located at (latitude, longitude) in degrees: (-77.6595,10.3135) to (-72.1207, -2.6817).
