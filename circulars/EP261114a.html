GCN Circular 46284: EP261114a: Einstein Probe detection of an X-ray transient

Event ID: EP261114a
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-15 03:08:54 UTC
Source file: 46284.json

C. K. Kan (HKU), Y. Wang (PMO; UCB), Y. J. Yi (BNU), H. Sun (NAO, CAS) on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:

We report on the detection of an X-ray transient by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission, designated EP261114a. The transient triggered EP-WXT (ID: 01709322536) at 2026-11-14T19:40:35.649 (UTC). The WXT position of the source is R.A. = 68.336 deg, DEC = -3.541 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 3 arcmin in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic).

A follow-up observation with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) was performed automatically. Within the WXT error circle, an uncatalogued X-ray source was detected at R.A. = 68.3384 deg, DEC = -3.5109 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 20 arcsec in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic).

Further information will be updated when the telemetry data is received.

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

GCN Circular 46291: EP261114a is likely a flaring star

Event ID: EP261114a
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-15 10:50:42 UTC
Source file: 46291.json

C. K. Kan (HKU), Y. Wang (PMO; UCB), Y. J. Yi (BNU), H. Sun (NAO, CAS) on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:

The EP-WXT detection of EP261114a (Kan et al., GCN [46284](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/46284)) is likely a stellar flare associated with the high proper motion star UCAC4 433-006766. The updated position from the FXT telemetry data analysis is R.A. = 68.3436 deg, DEC = -3.50425 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of about 10 arcsec (radius, 90% C.L. statistical and systematic). The estimated flux of the flare is around 1.4 x 10^(-10) erg/s/cm^2 in 0.5-4.0 keV, corresponding to an X-ray luminosity of around 5.6 x 10^(31) erg/s.

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

GCN Circular 46293: EP261114a: NOT Optical Upper Limits

Event ID: EP261114a
Submitter: Gregory Corcoran at University College Dublin <gregory.corcoran@ucdconnect.ie>
Created: 2026-11-15 12:11:29 UTC
Source file: 46293.json

G. Corcoran (UCD), D. B. Malesani (DAWN/NBI and Radboud), R. A. J. Eyles-Ferris (Leicester), P. G. Jonker (Radboud), A. A. Chrimes (Radboud), A. Caballero Almagro (NOT), A. S. Mårtensson (NOT) report on behalf of a larger collaboration

We observed the field of the Einstein Probe fast X-ray transient candidate EP261114a (Kan et al., GCN 46284; Kan et al., GCN 46291) using the ALFOSC camera on the Nordic Optical Telescope (NOT). Observations consisted of 3 x 300s exposures in SDSS r-band and 5 x 200s exposures in SDSS z-band and started at 03:54:01 UT on 2026-11-15 (8.2 hours post trigger).

We performed image subtraction on our stacked images against Legacy Survey templates and also inspected the stacked images manually. We do not detect any new source within the EP/FXT uncertainty region (Kan et al., GCN 46291) down to 3-sigma upper limits of:

r > 23.7 
z > 22.9

We also do not detect any new sources in the original EP/FXT uncertainty region (Kan et al., GCN 46284). Our upper limits are in AB magnitudes, calibrated using nearby stars from the PanSTARRS catalogue, and are not corrected for Galactic extinction.

Our non-detection of any new source is consistent with a stellar flare origin as suggested by Kan et al. (GCN 46291). In our images, however, UCAC4 433-006766 is saturated and we therefore cannot directly confirm any variability.
