GCN Circular 45619: GRB 260918A: SVOM detection of a burst

Event ID: GRB 260918A

Created: 2026-09-18 04:30:49 UTC
Source file: 45619.json

Donghua Zhao (NAOC), Clara Plasse (HKU), Yun Wang (UCB; PMO), Yehao Cheng(YNU), Diego Götz (CEA) report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:

At 2026-09-18T03:47:56 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 260918A (SVOM burst-id sb26091801).

The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was only detected by the Image Trigger (IMT), which produced a sequence of 2 alerts. IMT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 7.60 in the [8-120] keV energy band over a time window of 40.96 seconds starting at 2026-09-18T03:47:15.

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 354.8016, -8.9544 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 23h39m12.39s
Dec. (J2000) = -8d57m15.73s
 with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 10.33 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

SVOM slewed to the burst. 
The SVOM/MXT team is waiting for the full dataset to detect and localise the possible SVOM/MXT counterpart. 
SVOM/VT began observing the field after the slew. The observations will be reported in circulars.


The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. SVOM/ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC. SVOM/GRM was developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS. SVOM/MXT was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IJCLab, University of Leicester, MPE.

The Burst Advocate (BA) on shift for this alert is Yun Wang: wangyun@pmo.ac.cn.
Please contact the BA by email if you require additional information.

03:49:13 to 05:05:28 UTC (from 0.03 to 1.32 hours after the trigger) and obtained 76 minutes of exposure in the r and z filters.

r > 23.46,
z > 22.47.

Further observations are ongoing.

We observed the field of GRB 260918A (SVOM burst-id sb26091801) detected by SVOM (Zhao et al., GCN 45619) with the LCO 1m telescope at Teide Observatory equipped with the Sinistro instrument.
 
Our observation started on 2026-09-18 at 04:00:15.482 UT (about 12.3 min after the trigger) and we obtained 3x200 s exposures in the SDSS r and 3x200 s exposures in the Pan-STARRS z filters.

r > 22.1 AB (5-sigma, mid-time 17.76 min after the trigger);
z > 20.3 AB (5-sigma, mid-time 25.92 min after the trigger).

GCN Circular 45639: Fermi GBM Sub-Threshold Detection of GRB 260918A

Event ID: GRB 260918A
Submitter: Angus Jameson <abj0023@uah.edu>
Created: 2026-09-18 19:07:04 UTC
Source file: 45639.json

A. Jameson (UAH) reports on behalf of the Fermi-GBM Team:

SVOM ECLAIRs detected the GRB 260918A on 2016-09-18 at 03:47:36 UTC (GCN [45619](https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45619?view=index&query=260918A&startDate=&endDate=&sort=circularID)). There was no Fermi-GBM onboard trigger around this event time. An automated, blind search for gamma-ray bursts below the onboard triggering threshold in Fermi-GBM identified no candidates. 

The GBM Targeted Search [1], the most sensitive coherent search for GRB-like signals in GBM, identified a transient starting approximately 14 seconds after the ECLAIRs best image SNR time of 2026-09-18T03:47:15 UTC. The Targeted Search candidate was found most significantly on the 2.048 s timescale, with a false alarm rate of 1.3e-4 Hz. The Fermi-MET of the start time of this transient is 811396053.676 s. The Targeted Search localization has a spatial association probability of 71.3%, but has large  uncertainties due to the weak nature of the transient.
 
Additionally, the GBM Targeted Search event was found with the highest significance using a "hard" spectrum (Comptonized function with Epeak = 1500 keV, alpha = -0.5) for a GRB. 

The Targeted Search data release product for this event can be found here: https://fermi.gsfc.nasa.gov/ssc/data/access/gbm/targeted_search/trigger_report.php?trigger_id=811396040&sample=SVOM_GCNs&hash=e9806eaf891b7de8

[1] Goldstein et al. 2019 [arXiv:1903.12597](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.12597)

Y.Y. Shi (GXU), Z. Kang (CHO), C. Wu , L.P. Xin, X.H. Han, P.P. Zhang, X.M. Lu (NAOC), Z.W. Li, Y. Lv (CHO), R.S. Zhang, Y.J. Xiao, Y.L. Qiu, J. Wang, J.S. Deng, L. Huang, J.Y. Wei (NAOC), Yun Wang (UCB; PMO), Yehao Cheng(YNU) report on behalf of the SVOM/C-GFT team:

 
We observed the field of GRB 260918A detected by SVOM/Eclairs (Zhao et al., GCN 45619) with LATIOS on SVOM/C-GFT. Our observations started at 2026-09-18T16:11:08 UTC, ~ 12.39 hr after the trigger. 
 
We obtained follow-up imaging in g, r, and i bands. After preliminary analysis, no credible candidate was detected within the error box of source #1 and #2 provided by EP-FXT (Zou et al., GCN 45632) in our images. We measure the following upper limits calibrated against the Pan-STARRS DR1 catalog, and not corrected for Galactic extinction:
 
g > 20.5 AB (3-sigma, mid-time 13.56 hr after the trigger).
r > 21.1 AB (3-sigma, mid-time 13.24 hr after the trigger).
i > 20.5 AB (3-sigma, mid-time 12.67 hr after the trigger).
 
Our non-detection is consistent with the non-detections by Méndez et al. (GCN 45620), Yin et al. (GCN 45622), Li et al. (GCN 45634). 

We note that in source #3 position of EP/FXT (Zou et al., GCN 45632), a known optical source (GR*244: Double or Multiple Star) is bright in PS1 DR1; in source #4 position, a known optical source (6dFGS gJ233834.0-090039 : Seyfert 1 Galaxy) is ~0.6 mag brighter than in the catalogue in b band.


We thank the observation assistants C.-L. Guo and Y.-H. Hao at Jilin observatory for their excellent support.
 
The Chinese Ground Follow-up Telescope (C-GFT) for the SVOM mission is located at Jilin Station, Changchun Observatory, National Astronomical Observatories, CAS. It features two instruments: (1) CATCH at the Cassegrain focus with a 21 arcmin x 21 arcmin FOV for simultaneous g/r/i-band imaging, and (2) LATIOS, a 4k x 4k CMOS camera at the prime focus with a 1.28 deg x 1.28 deg FOV that images in g, r, and i bands via filter switching.

GCN Circular 45692: GRB 260918A: SVOM/ECLAIRs refined analysis

M. Brunet, H. Yang, O.Godet (IRAP), Yun Wang (UCB; PMO), Yehao Cheng(YNU)
report on behalf of the SVOM/ECLAIRs team

Using the event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station, we report further analysis of SVOM/ECLAIRs observations of GRB 260918A (SVOM burst-id sb26091801 – GCN 45619, trigger time T0 = 2026-09-18T03:47:56 UTC), which was also detected as a subthreshold by Fermi/GBM (GCN 45639). 

The burst that triggered ECLAIRs onboard shows a multiple peak lightcurve. The burst duration is T90 = 82 +28/-5s  in the 4-120 keV energy band.

The refined burst localization is RA, Dec = 354.725, -8.909 degrees (J2000) 

RA (J2000) = 23h38m54.0s
Dec (J2000) = -08d54m32.4s 

with a 90% C.L. radius of 8.73 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

The time-averaged spectrum from T0-34 s to T0+36 s in the 5-120 keV energy range is best fitted by a powerlaw, model with alpha = -1.26 +0.12/-0.11. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is  (8.9 +/-0.9)e-7 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is 0.29 +/- 0.3 ph/cm^2/s. 

All the quoted errors are at the 68% confidence level.
