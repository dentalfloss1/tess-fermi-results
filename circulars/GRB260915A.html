GCN Circular 45570: GRB 260915A: SVOM detection of a burst

Y. F. Liang(PMO), C. Plasse (HKU), H. Goto (RIKEN/CEA), W. Xie (NAOC), W. Tan (IHEP), Y. N. Ma (NAOC) report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:

At 2026-09-15T01:12:03 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 260915A (SVOM burst-id sb26091501). The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was detected both by the Count-Rate Trigger (CRT) and the Image Trigger (IMT), which produced a sequence of 14 alerts. IMT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 21.38 in the [8-120] keV energy band over a time window of 40.96 seconds starting at 2026-09-15T01:12:00.

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 8.1021, 8.2587 degrees (J2000) with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 4.12 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

This burst was also detected by SVOM/GRM with a significance of 8.

SVOM slewed to the burst.

SVOM/MXT began observing the field at 2026-09-15T01:14:42 UTC, 159 seconds after T0. Using onboard processed data we found an uncatalogued X-ray source located at R.A., Dec. 8.1504, 8.2440 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 0h32m36.09s
Dec. (J2000) = 8d14m38.47s
with a 90% C.L. radius of 49.74 arcseconds.

This location is 3.00 arcminutes from the ECLAIRs onboard position. This position may be improved as more data is received.

VT began observing the field after the slew. The analysis of the data will be published in a future circular.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. SVOM/ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC. SVOM/GRM was developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS. SVOM/MXT was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IJCLab, University of Leicester, MPE.

SVOM ECLAIRs detected GRB 260915A on 2026-09-15 at 01:12:03 UTC (GCN 45570). There was no Fermi-GBM onboard trigger around this time.

The GBM Targeted Search [1], the most sensitive coherent search for GRB-like signals in GBM, identified a spatially-consistent transient at 2026-09-15T01:12:01.517 UTC. The Targeted Search event is found most significantly on the 32.768 s timescale with a false alarm rate of 8.3e-5 Hz. The Fermi-MET of this transient is 811127526.517 s. GCN Circular 45612: GRB 260915A: SVOM/ECLAIRs refined analysis

Event ID: GRB 260915A
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-09-17 12:22:19 UTC

H. Yang, M. Brunet, O. Godet (IRAP), W. J. Xie (NAOC), Y. F. Liang (PMO), C. Plasse (HKU), H. Goto (RIKEN/CEA) report on behalf of the SVOM/ECLAIRs team:

Using the event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station, we report further analysis of SVOM/ECLAIRs observations of GRB 260915A (SVOM burst-id sb26091501 – GCN 45570, trigger time T0 = 2026-09-15T01:12:03 UTC), which was also detected by Fermi/GBM as a sub-threshold (Hamburg, GCN 45574).

The burst that triggered ECLAIRs onboard shows a broad bump from T0 - 14 s to T0 + 200 s in the 4-120 keV energy band, with a slew performed from T0 + 42 s to T0 + 135 s. The burst duration estimated through the imaging technique is T90 = (167 +10/-6) s in the 4-120 keV energy band. 

The time-averaged spectrum from T0 - 14 s to T0 + 42 s (before the slew) in the 5-120 keV band is best fitted by a power-law model, with alpha= -1.20 +/- 0.05. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is (2.6 +/- 0.1)e-6 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is (9.9 +/- 0.4)e-1 ph/cm^2/s. 

The time-averaged spectrum from T0 + 135 s to T0 + 200 s (after the slew) in the 5-120 keV band is best fitted by a cutoffpl model, with alpha = -1.4 +0.4/-0.8 and Epeak = 54 +80/-24 keV. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is (2.7 +/- 0.6)e-7 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is (1.4 +0.3/-0.2)e-1  ph/cm^2/s. 

All the quoted errors are at the 68% confidence level.
