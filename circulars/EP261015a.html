GCN Circular 45955: EP261015a (WXT 11900947714): Possible optical counterpart from NOT and LCO

Event ID: EP261015a
Submitter: Gregory Corcoran at University College Dublin <gregory.corcoran@ucdconnect.ie>
Created: 2026-10-16 05:15:19 UTC
Source file: 45955.json

G. Corcoran (UCD), D. B. Malesani (DAWN/NBI and Radboud), P. G. Jonker (Radboud), J. Chacon (PUC), and F. E. Bauer (SSI and UTA), O. Stalder (NOT) report on behalf of a larger collaboration:

We observed the field of the Einstein Probe fast X-ray transient EP261015a (WXT trigger 11900947714) using the ALFOSC camera on the Nordic Optical Telescope (NOT). Observations consisted of a sequence of 3 x 300 s exposures in the SDSS r-band, and started on 2026-10-16 at 02:58:58 UT (13.72 hr after the EP/WXT trigger).

After performing image subtraction against Dark Energy Survey stacked images, we identify a potential counterpart at coordinates (J2000):

RA: 02:57:19.83
Dec: -13:21:17.3

We measure a difference magnitude of r = 22.74 +/- 0.21 (AB) at a midtime of 2026-10-16 03:06:55 UT (13.86 hr after the EP/WXT trigger), calibrated using nearby stars in the Pan-STARRS catalog and not corrected for Galactic extinction.

At a similar mid-time (2026-10-16 03:02:53 UT), we performed additional observations using an LCO 1m telescope located at the Cerro Tololo Interamerican Observatory (Chile) equipped with the SINISTRO instrument. Here observations consisted of 9 x 300 s exposures in SDSS r-band. We see the above candidate source in our difference image as well, at r = 22.83 +/- 0.16 (AB).

We caution it is possible that this source is an artefact of the image subtraction due to presence of an underlying galaxy, however the source’s detection from both telescopes and clean residuals in the photometry lend credence to it being real. Given our two observations are at similar times, we cannot make a useful estimate of its temporal evolution.

GCN Circular 45956: EP261015a: Einstein Probe detection of a fast X-ray transient

Event ID: EP261015a
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-10-16 06:21:08 UTC
Source file: 45956.json

R.-Z. Li (YNAO), G. -L. Huang, Z.-X. Li (IHEP), M. Nie (YNAO) and H. Sun (NAOC) on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:

We report on the detection of a fast X-ray transient by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission, designated EP261015a. The transient did not trigger EP-WXT onboard. We sent a GCN Notice manually with trigger ID: 11900947714. The WXT light curve of the transient began at 2026-10-15T13:15:36 UTC, exhibited two peaks, and lasted for 130 s. The WXT position of the source is R.A. = 44.341 deg, DEC = -13.365 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 3 arcmin in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic).

The 0.5-4 keV WXT  spectrum can be fitted by an absorbed powerlaw model with a fixed Galactic column density of 3.5 x 10^20 cm^-2 and a  photon index of 1.45 (-0.71,+0.88). The derived 0.5-4 keV unabsorbed flux is 6.25 (-2.91/+3.90) x 10^-10 erg/cm^2/s.

A follow-up observation with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) has been scheduled. Further information will be updated when the telemetry data is received.

A possible optical counterpart to the transient was reported by NOT and LCO (Corcoran et al., GCN 45955).

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

GCN Circular 45969: EP261015a: refined analysis of EP-FXT observations

R.-Z. Li (YNAO), Z.-X. Li, G.-L. Huang (IHEP), M. Nie (YNAO) and H. Sun (NAOC) on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:

The X-ray transient EP261015a was detected by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) onboard the Einstein Probe (EP) mission at 2026-10-15T13:15:36 UTC (Li et al., GCN 45956). Follow-up observations with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) onboard EP began at 2026-10-16T04:31:12 UTC, approximately 15.26 hours after the WXT trigger, with an exposure time of 3231 s.

We stacked the FXT-A and FXT-B data. Within the EP/WXT error circle (Li et al., GCN 45956), no X-ray source was detected at the position of the optical candidate (Corcoran et al., GCN 45955; Méndez et al., GCN 45960; Ma et al., GCN 45963; Mohan et al., GCN 45967). The derived 0.5–10 keV flux upper limit is approximately 1.33 × 10^-13 erg cm^-2 s^-1 (90% confidence level).

An uncatalogued X-ray source was detected in the stacked data within the EP/WXT error circle (Li et al., GCN 45956). Its position is R.A. = 44.3459 deg, Decl. = −13.3915 deg (J2000), with a positional uncertainty of 10 arcsec in radius (90% confidence level, including statistical and systematic uncertainties). The 0.5-10 keV FXT spectrum of this source can be fitted with an absorbed power-law model, with the Galactic hydrogen column density fixed at 3.5 × 10^20 cm^-2 and a photon index of 1.55 (+1.45, −1.43). The derived unabsorbed 0.5–10 keV flux is 7.01 (+6.27, −3.45) × 10^-14 erg cm^-2 s^-1. All quoted uncertainties on the spectral parameters and fluxes are at the 90% confidence level.

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space-based X-ray observatory designed to monitor the soft X-ray sky and perform X-ray follow-up observations (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

GCN Circular 45972: EP261015a: Tentative fading of the candidate optical counterpart from the NOT

Event ID: EP261015a
Submitter: Gregory Corcoran at University College Dublin <gregory.corcoran@ucdconnect.ie>
Created: 2026-10-17 16:03:10 UTC
Source file: 45972.json

G. Corcoran (UCD), D. B. Malesani (DAWN/NBI and Radboud), A. Martin-Carrillo (UCD), P. G. Jonker (Radboud) A. L. Bouquin (NOT), A. Caballero Almagro (NOT), E. Georgiou (NOT and Turku Univ.) report on behalf of a larger collaboration

We observed the field of the Einstein Probe fast X-ray transient EP261015a (Li et al., GCN 45956) using the ALFOSC camera on the Nordic Optical Telescope (NOT). Observations consisted of a sequence of 5 x 300 s exposures in SDSS-r band and started on 2026-10-17 at 02:12:26 UT (1.54 days after event start). Due to the telescope pointing and dither pattern, there are some artifacts caused by a bright star to the north of the target that affect part of our images. 

After dropping two images affected by the bright star and performing image subtraction against a Dark Energy Survey stacked image, we clearly detect the candidate discussed by Corcoran et al. (GCN 45955), Mendez et al (GCN 45960), and Ma et al. (GCN 45963). We measure a difference magnitude of r = 23.18 +/- 0.10 (AB) calibrated against nearby stars in the Pan-STARRS catalog and not corrected for Galactic extinction at a midtime of 2026-10-17 02:20:22 UT (1.54 days after event start). 

We also refined our photometry on the previous epoch presented in GCN 45955 to r = 22.64 +/- 0.12. This shows fading between epochs with a decay of t^{-0.51+/-0.15}. We note that the uncertainties on our photometry neglect the difficult to estimate systematic uncertainties inherent to image subtraction between instruments, therefore the statistical significance of the fading cannot be reliably determined.

The nature of this object is still not fully clear. An X-ray source has been reported by EP/FXT within the EP/WXT error circle (Li et al., GCN 45969), positionally inconsistent with the above candidate. This source is however the likely counterpart of a spectroscopically confirmed QSO (https://www.legacysurvey.org/viewer/desi-spectrum/dr1/targetid39627462111402716), thus likely unrelated to the EP/WXT event (no significant variability is detected in the QSO flux between our two epochs). The optical fading would then favor an association of the candidate with EP261015a, though an unrelated transient (e.g. a SN) cannot be fully discarded.

Further monitoring is planned and encouraged to clarify the nature of this object.
