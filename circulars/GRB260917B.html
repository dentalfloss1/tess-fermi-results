GCN Circular 45606: GRB 260917B: SVOM detection of a faint burst

Event ID: GRB 260917B
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-09-17 04:33:30 UTC
Source file: 45606.json

Hanrui He (PMO), Wenjin Xie (NAOC), Chao Wu (NAOC), Kuan Liu (USTC), Clara Plasse (HKU) , Hatsune Goto (RIKEN, CEA), Stephane Schanne (CEA)  report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:

At 2026-09-17T03:52:21 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 260917B (SVOM burst-id sb26091701). The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was detected both by the Count-Rate Trigger (CRT) and the Image Trigger (IMT), which produced a sequence of 8 alerts. CRT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 8.95 in the [8-120] keV energy band over a time window of 20.40 seconds starting at 2026-09-17T03:52:11.

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 46.8646, 25.4836 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 3h07m27.50s
Dec. (J2000) = 25d29m00.99s
 with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 8.84 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

SVOM slewed to the burst. MXT and VT began observing the field after the slew. The analysis of the data will be published in a future circular.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. SVOM/ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC. SVOM/GRM was developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS. SVOM/MXT was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IJCLab, University of Leicester, MPE.

The Burst Advocate (BA) on shift for this alert is Chao Wu: cwu@bao.ac.cn
Please contact the BA by email if you require additional information.

Watson (UNAM), Hanrui He (PMO), and Wenjin Xie (NAOC) report:

We imaged the field of the SVOM GRB 260917B (He et al., GCN Circ. 45606) using the DDRAGO two-channel wide-field imager on the SVOM/COLIBRÍ (FM-GFT) telescope. We observed from 2026-09-17 05:24:09 to 2026-09-17 05:33:40 UTC (from 1.53 to 1.69 hours after the trigger) and obtained 16 minutes of simultaneous exposure in the r/z filters.

The data were reduced, coadded and analyzed with the COLIBRÍ pipeline. The photometry was calibrated using nearby stars from the PanSTARRS DR1, is in the AB system, and is not corrected for Galactic extinction.

We detect an uncatalogued and fading source consistent with the ECLAIRs 8.84 arcmin error circle (He et al., GCN Circ. 45606) at: 

RA(J2000) = 03:07:56.12 = 46.98383 degrees
Dec(J2000) = +25:33:24.4 = 25.55678 degrees
with an uncertainty of 0.5 arcsec.

The preliminary magnitude derived for that source is:

r = 21.29 +/- 0.06
z = 19.84 +/- 0.06

Further observations are ongoing.

We thank the staff of the Observatorio Astronómico Nacional at Sierra de San Pedro Mártir, as well as the technical and engineering teams at CEA, CPPM, IRAP, LAM, OHP, OSU Pytheas, and UNAM.

COLIBRÍ is an astronomical observatory developed and operated jointly by France (AMU, CNES and CNRS) and Mexico (UNAM and SECIHTI). It is located at the Observatorio Astronómico Nacional on the Sierra de San Pedro Mártir, Baja California, Mexico.

SVOM/VT performed an automatic slew on the burst GRB 260917B (sb26091701) triggered by SVOM/ECLAIRs (He et al., GCN 45606). SVOM/VT began observing the field at 2026-09-17T05:27:45 UTC, 1.59 hours after T0, in the VT_B (400nm-650nm) and VT_R (650nm-1000nm) channels simultaneously. 

With X-band data available, the SVOM/COLIBRÍ optical candidate (Dornic et al., GCN 45607; Yin et al., GCN 45608) was detected in both VT_B and VT_R bands and showed a power law decay with the index of 1.0 during our observation. The AB magnitudes are:

 mid time  | exposure time  | band | mag (AB) 
-----------|----------------|------|------------
   2.83 h  |     22*100 s   | VT_B |  22.7+/-0.2   
   2.85 h  |     22*100 s   | VT_R |  20.8+/-0.1

Our photometry was not corrected for Galactic extinction.

Given the red VT color (VT_B-VT_R~1.9) of this burst, it is likely at medium redshift (Wang et al., 2020).

We observed the field of GRB 260917B (He et al., GCN 45606) in the near-infrared J band with the Palomar 1-m telescope, equipped with the 1.2-square degree WINTER camera (Lourie et al. 2020, Frostig et al. 2024).

Observations began on 2026-09-17 at 06:18:01.556 UTC in the J band (2.43 hr after the SVOM/ECLAIRs trigger), consisting of 15*120 sec exposures. The images were processed using the WINTER data reduction pipeline implemented with mirar (https://github.com/winter-telescope/mirar, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.13352565).

We detect a faint source present at the optical counterpart position (Dornic et al., GCN 45607; Yin et al., GCN 45608; Ma et al., GCN 45610) in our stacked image. 
We obtain the following magnitude: J = 19.02 +- 0.23 mag (AB). 

WINTER (Wide-field INfrared Transient ExploreR) is a partnership between MIT and Caltech, housed at Palomar Observatory, and funded by NSF MRI, NSF AAG, the David and Lucile Packard Foundation, and the MIT Kavli Institute for Astrophysics and Space Research.

Martin Jelinek, Jan Strobl and Filip Novotny

Martin Masek, Sergey Karpov, Petr Janecek, Jakub Jurysek, 
Jan Ebr, Ronan Cunniffe, Petr Travnicek and Michael Prouza 
(Institute of Physics, Prague, CZ)

report:

The 30cm robotic telescope FRAM-Auger at the Pierre Auger
Observatory (Cerro Los Leones, Malargue, Argentina) reacted
robotically to the alert of GRB 260917B (He et al., GCN
45606), obtaining a series of R-band images starting at
04:04:32 UT, i.e. ~12 min post trigger, at an elevation of
only 7-9 deg. A second series was obtained about two hours
after the trigger, when the field had risen higher.

We do not detect the optical counterpart reported by Dornic
et al. (GCN 45607), Yin et al. (GCN 45608), Ma et al. (GCN
45610) and Mohan et al. (GCN 45611), neither in single
images nor in the combined frames, with the following
limits:

T-T0[mid]  Images  Span[s]  Limit (Vega)
~17 min    9       630      R_C > 17.0
~116 min   11      767      R_C > 17.9

The photometry is calibrated against the Atlas-REFCAT2
catalogue (Tonry et al. 2018, ApJ, 867, 105) and is not
corrected for Galactic extinction. The early limit implies
that this faint burst was not accompanied by a bright
optical flash or strong flaring activity.

GCN Circular 45681: GRB 260917B:: SVOM/ECLAIRs refined analysis

Event ID: GRB 260917B
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-09-21 14:58:07 UTC
Source file: 45681.json

H. Yang, M. Brunet, O. Godet (IRAP), N. Dagoneau (CEA), K. Liu (USTC), C. Wu (NAOC)
report on behalf of the SVOM/ECLAIRs team:

Using the event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station, we report further analysis of SVOM/ECLAIRs observations of GRB 260917B (SVOM burst-id sb26091701 – GCN 45606, trigger time T0 = 2026-09-17T03:52:21 UTC).

The burst that triggered ECLAIRs onboard shows a broad bump from T0 - 18 s to T0 + 17 s. The burst duration estimated through the imaging technique is T90 =  35 +/- 6 s in the 4-120 keV energy band. 

The time-averaged spectrum from T0 - 18 s to T0 + 17 s in the 5-120 keV energy range is best fitted by a powerlaw model, with alpha = -1.4 +/- 0.1. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is (7.4 +/- 0.8)e-7 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is (5.7 +/- 0.5)e-1 ph/cm^2/s. 

All the quoted errors are at the 68% confidence level.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC.
