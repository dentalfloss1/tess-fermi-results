GCN Circular 45895: GRB 261011A: SVOM detection of a long bright burst

Event ID: GRB 261011A
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-10-11 14:20:12 UTC
Source file: 45895.json

Yiming Huang (APC), Massimiliano Lincetto (CPPM), Damien Dornic (CPPM), Hui Yang (IRAP), Pierre Maggi (ObAS)  report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:
At 2026-10-11T13:49:04 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 261011A (SVOM burst-id sb26101102).

The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was detected both by the Count-Rate Trigger (CRT) and the Image Trigger (IMT), which produced a sequence of 22 alerts. IMT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 90.76 in the [8-120] keV energy band over a time window of 40.96 seconds starting at 2026-10-11T13:49:04.

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 21.9017, -21.5422 degrees (J2000) with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 2.17 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

The SVOM/ECLAIRs light curve showed a multiple peak structure with a T90 duration of about 66.77 (-1.48 +1.35) s.

This burst also triggered SVOM/GRM at 2026-10-11T13:49:07 on a timescale of 1 seconds with an SNR of 7. The SVOM/GRM light curve showed a multiple peak structure with a T90 duration of about 63.09 (-0.74 +0.67) s.

SVOM slewed to the burst.

SVOM/MXT began observing the field at 2026-10-11T13:56:38 UTC, 454 seconds after T0. Using onboard processed data we found an uncatalogued X-ray source located at R.A., Dec. 21.9074, -21.5577 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 1h27m37.79s
Dec. (J2000) = -21d33m27.83s
with a 90% C.L. radius of 60.42 arcseconds.

This location is 0.99 arcminutes from the ECLAIRs onboard position. This position may be improved as more data is received.

VT began observing the field after the slew. The analysis of the data will be published in a future circular.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. SVOM/ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC. SVOM/GRM was developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS. SVOM/MXT was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IJCLab, University of Leicester, MPE.

The Burst Advocate (BA) on shift for this alert is Yiming HUANG: huangym@ihep.ac.cn.
Please contact the BA by email if you require additional information.

GCN Circular 45896: GRB 261011A: Swift detection of an unusual burst

S. B. Cenko (GSFC), S. D. Barthelmy (GSFC), S. Dichiara (PSU),
R. Gupta (NASA GSFC), N. P. M. Kuin (UCL-MSSL), A. Y. Lien (U Tampa),
M. J. Moss (GSFC), K. L. Page (U Leicester), D. M. Palmer (LANL), 
and M. H. Siegel (PSU) report on behalf of the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory Team:

At 13:46:41 UT, the Swift Burst Alert Telescope (BAT) triggered and
located GRB 261011A (trigger=1516664).  Swift slewed immediately to the burst.
The BAT on-board calculated location is 
RA, Dec 21.925, -21.529 which is 
   RA(J2000) = 01h 27m 42s
   Dec(J2000) = -21d 31' 42"
with an uncertainty of 3 arcmin (radius, 90% containment, including 
systematic uncertainty).  The BAT light curve showed a broad soft pulse 
from T0 to T0+130 sec and a shoulder extending out to T0+260 sec for a total 
duration of about 260 sec.  The peak count rate was ~8000 counts/sec 
(15-350 keV), at ~95 sec after the trigger.

The XRT began observing the field at 13:51:57.1 UT, 315.8 seconds after
the BAT trigger. No source was detected in the 2.5-s promptly available
image. We are waiting for the full dataset to detect and localise the
XRT counterpart.

UVOT took a finding chart exposure of nominal 150.000 seconds with the White
filter starting 321 seconds after the BAT trigger. There is a candidate
afterglow in the list of sources generated on-board at
  RA(J2000)  =	01:27:35.39 =  21.89746
  DEC(J2000) = -21:33:37.2  = -21.56032
with a 90%-confidence error radius of about 1.10 arc sec. This position is
145.7 arc sec. from the center of the BAT error circle. The estimated magnitude
is 17.78. No correction has been made for extinction.

The BAT lightcurve is very unusual in that it combines a 260 second long
low energy emission mostly below 50 keV, with a ~5 second short peak
structure around T+95 seconds visible at all energies up the to 100-350 keV band.

Burst Advocate for this burst is S. B. Cenko (brad.cenko AT nasa.gov).
Please contact the BA by email if you require additional information
regarding Swift followup of this burst. In extremely urgent cases, after
trying the Burst Advocate, you can contact the Swift PI by phone (see
Swift TOO web site for information: http://www.swift.psu.edu/)

GCN Circular 45897: GRB 261011A: GOTO optical counterpart detection

D. O'Neill,B. P. Gompertz, M. Shrestha, R. Starling, A. Kumar, G. Ramsay, K. Ackley, M. Dyer, J. Lyman, K. Ulaczyk, D. Steeghs, D. Galloway, V. Dhillon, P. O'Brien, K. Noysena, R. Kotak, R. Breton, J. Casares, L. Nuttall, B. Godson, T. Killestein and M. Pursiainen report on behalf of GOTO collaboration:

We report on optical observations with the Gravitational-wave Optical Transient Observer (GOTO; Steeghs et al. 2022, Dyer et al. 2024) in response to the Swift/BAT (Cenko et al. GCN 45896 and SVOM ECLIARS (Huang et al, GCN 45895) alerts for GRB 261011A.  

Observations covering the localisation area began at 2026-10-11 13:53:16 UT, (+0.11h post trigger) with an average 5-sigma depth of 21.2 mag.

Images were processed immediately after acquisition using the GOTO pipeline. Difference imaging was performed using deeper template observations. Source candidates were initially filtered using a classifier (Killestein et al. 2021) and cross-matched against a variety of contextual and minor planet catalogs. Human vetting was carried out in real time on any candidates that passed the above checks (Lyman et al. 2026).

We detect the counterpart reported in Cenko et al. GCN 45896.The source was detected with L = 16.32 ± 0.01 AB mag (+0.11h). We find no evidence of the source prior to the GRB trigger time in previous GOTO observations taken at 2026-10-11 13:40:24 (-0.1h) down to a depth of >20.57 AB mag, the ZTF observations provided by the Lasair broker (Smith et al. 2019), or the ATLAS forced photometry server (Shingles et al. 2021).

A. Aryan, Z.-Y. Chen, T.-W. Chen, W.-J. Hou, Y.-H. Lee (all NCU), A. K. H. Kong (NTHU), S. Yang (HNAS), S. J. Smartt, J. H. Gillanders (both Oxford), A. Sankar.K, K. N.-T. Ho, A. Dutta, M.-H. Lee, C.-S. Lin, Y.-C. Pan, C.-C. Ngeow, C.-H. Lai, H.-C. Lin, H.-Y. Hsiao, J.-K. Guo (all NCU), Z. N. Wang, D. C. Qiang, L. L. Fan (all HNAS), Y. J. Yang (NYUAD), H.-W. Lin (UMich), K. W. Smith, H. F. Stevance, S. Srivastav, L. Rhodes (all Oxford), M. Nicholl, M. Fulton, C. Angus, A. Aamer (all QUB), T. Moore (STScI), A. Schultz and M. Huber (both IfA, Hawaii) report:

As part of the Kinder collaboration (Chen et al. 2025, ApJ, 983, 86,doi:10.3847/1538-4357/adb428), we conducted multi-band optical follow-up observations of GRB 261011A, detected by SVOM (Huang et al., GCN 45895) and Swift (Cenko et al., GCN 45896).

Imaging was acquired in the g-band using the 1m Lulin One-meter Telescope (LOT), and in the r- and i-bands using the 40cm Seisi Lulin Telescope (SLT). Our observations started at MJD 61324.6155, approximately 0.99 hr after the Swift/BAT trigger.

The optical counterpart reported by Swift/UVOT (Cenko et al., GCN 45896), GOTO (O'Neill et al., GCN 45897), and more instruments (Fu et al., GCN 45898; Izzo et al., GCN 45899; Wu et al., GCN 45900; Yin et al., GCN 45901; Shi et al., GCN 45902) was clearly detected in all three bands.

We used AutoPhOT (Brennan & Fraser 2022, A&A, 667, A62) to perform PSF photometry. Preliminary measurements are as follows:

Telescope | Filter | MJD (start) | t-t0 (hr) | Exposure (s) | Magnitude
LOT       | g      | 61324.6155  | 0.99      | 60 * 1       | 20.47 +/- 0.10
SLT       | r      | 61324.6157  | 1.04      | 300 * 1      | 19.91 +/- 0.07
SLT       | i      | 61324.6194  | 1.09      | 300 * 1      | 19.69 +/- 0.07

Using the nearly simultaneous g- and r-band measurements at approximately 1.04 hr after the trigger, and correcting for Galactic foreground extinction, we obtain (g-r) = 0.64 +/- 0.11 mag. Using the earliest r- and i-band measurements, we obtain (r-i) = 0.20 +/- 0.10 mag. Thus, the source shows a relatively red (g-r) colour while remaining considerably flatter between r and i.

The presented magnitudes are calibrated using field stars from the ATLAS-RefCat2 catalog from MAST (Tonry et al. 2018, ApJ, 867, 105) and are not corrected for Galactic foreground extinction of A_g = 0.07, A_r = 0.05 and A_i = 0.04 mag in the direction of the transient (Schlafly & Finkbeiner 2011).  

The methodology, details on the Lulin Observatory telescopes, and a compilation of our optical follow-up campaign for FXTs discovered within the first year of operation of the Einstein Probe mission are presented in Aryan et al. 2025, ApJS, 281, 20, doi:10.3847/1538-4365/adfc69.

Chenxi Bao (PKU), Yacheng Kang (PKU), Xinmiao Zhao (PKU), and Zhuokai Liu (PKU) report on behalf of the Interestar Collaboration and the PKU HiTF (High-energy Transients Follow-up) group:

We observed the field of GRB 261011A, detected by Swift/BAT (Cenko et al., GCN 45896; T0 = 2026-10-11T13:46:41) and SVOM/ECLAIRs (Huang et al, GCN 45895), using a 28cm optical telescope located in Chengde, Hebei, China. The observation began on 2026-10-11 at 14:33:32 UT, approximately 0.78 hr after the Swift/BAT trigger. 

We obtained the Luminance-band image with a total exposure time of 1800 s. No significant source is detected at the position of the reported optical counterpart (Cenko et al., GCN 45896; O'Neill et al., GCN 45897; Fu et al., GCN 45898; Izzo et al., GCN 45899; Wu et al., GCN 45900; Yin et al., GCN 45901; Shi et al., GCN 45903; Aryan et al., GCN 45904; Buckley et al., GCN 45905). We derive a 5-sigma upper limit of L > 19.65 mag. 

Preliminary photometric calibration was performed using nearby catalogued field stars. The reported limit is an approximate AB-equivalent magnitude and has not been corrected for Galactic extinction. Our non-detection is consistent with the reported fading behavior of the optical counterpart. Further optical follow-up observations are encouraged.

The 28cm optical telescope is operated by the Interestar Collaboration, which contributes to the rapid follow-up of high-energy transients as part of the PKU HiTF group.
