GCN Circular 45534: GRB 260909A: SVOM detection of a burst

Event ID: GRB 260909A
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>

Source file: 45534.json

A. Saccardi, S. Schanne (CEA/Irfu), Y.-H. I. Yin,  C. Plasse (HKU), Y. Huang (APC), B. Cordier, J. T. Palmerio (CEA/Irfu) report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:

At 2026-09-09T11:18:01 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 260909A (SVOM burst-id sb26090910).

The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was detected both by the Count-Rate Trigger (CRT) and the Image Trigger (IMT), which produced a sequence of 7 alerts. CRT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 12.10 in the [8-50] keV energy band over a time window of 20.40 seconds starting at 2026-09-09T11:17:45 (Tb).

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 13.0009, 24.2188 degrees (J2000) with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 6.67 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

This burst also triggered SVOM/GRM at 2026-09-09T11:18:01 on a timescale of 1 seconds with an SNR of 7.30.
The SVOM/GRM light curve showed a single peak structure with a duration of about 30 s.

SVOM slewed to the burst.

SVOM/MXT began observing the field at 2026-09-09T11:21:14 UTC, 193 seconds after T0. Using onboard processed data we found an uncatalogued X-ray source located at R.A., Dec. 13.0346, 24.1956 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 0h52m08s
Dec. (J2000) = 24d11m44s
with a 90% C.L. radius of 64 arcseconds.

This location is 2.31 arcminutes from the ECLAIRs onboard position. This position may be improved as more data is received.

VT began observing the field after the slew. The analysis of the data will be published in a future circular.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. SVOM/ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC. SVOM/GRM was developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS. SVOM/MXT was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IJCLab, University of Leicester, MPE.

Using the event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station, we report further analysis of SVOM/ECLAIRs observations of GRB 260909A (SVOM burst-id sb26090910 – GCN 45534, trigger time T0 = 2026-09-09T11:18:01 UTC).

The burst that triggered ECLAIRs onboard shows a multiple peak lightcurve. The burst duration estimated through the imaging technique is T90 = 47 +2/-3 s in the 4-120 keV energy band.

The time-averaged spectrum from T0-16 s to T0+31 s in the 5-120 keV energy range is best fitted by a powerlaw model, with alpha = -1.23 +/-0.07. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is (9.4 +/-0.8)e-6 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is 4.5 +/-0.3 ph/cm^2/s. 

All the quoted errors are at the 68% confidence level.

I. Perez-Garcia, J. Rodrigo, M.D. Caballero-Garcia, M. Gritsevich, S.-Y. Wu, S. Guziy (IAA-CSIC), Y.-D. Hu (GXU), G. Gomez-Velarde (IAC, GRANTECAN), F. Tinaut-Ruano (CAHA) and A.J. Castro-Tirado (IAA-CSIC), on behalf of a larger collaboration

Following the detection of GRB 260909A by SVOM/ECLAIRs (Saccardi et al. GCN 45534), we triggered the 2.2m CAHA telescope (+ CAFOS) at the Calar Alto Observatory (Almeria, Spain). Images were gathered starting on Sep. 10 00:07 UT (i.e. ~12.8 hours after the trigger) in the Sloan i, Sloan z and clear bands, covering the SVOM/MXT (Saccardi et al. GCN 45534) and EP/FXT (Mao et al. GCN 45543) error circles. No optical transient is detected in the co-added images (900 s per band) down to 3-sigma limits of z = 21.13, i = 22.25 and clear = 22.43 mag.

The field was also observed with the 1.5m telescope at the Sierra Nevada Observatory (OSN, Granada, Spain). A 300 s I-band image was obtained on Sep. 10 starting at 01:49 UT (i.e. ~14.5 hours after the trigger). No optical transient is detected within the above-mentioned error boxes down to I = 20.61 mag (3-sigma).

Later on we also triggered the 10.4m GTC telescope equipped with OSIRIS in La Palma (Spain). Sloan z-band images were gathered on Sep. 10 starting at 05:33 UT (mid-time ~18.5 hours after the trigger). No optical transient is detected on the co-added image (900 s) down to z = 24.55 mag (3-sigma).

A second GTC/OSIRIS epoch in the Sloan z band was obtained on Sep. 12 (mid-time Sep. 12 03:16 UT, i.e. ~2.7 days after the trigger; 1020 s in total) under worse seeing conditions. No optical transient is detected down to z = 24.17 mag (3-sigma), and no variable source is found between both GTC epochs.

Magnitudes are in the AB system (Vega for the I band), calibrated against ATLAS-REFCAT2 (the clear band against Gaia G and the I band through the Lupton 2005 transformations), and not corrected for Galactic extinction.

R.A. = 00:52:08.462
Dec. = +24:11:47.12

With an uncertainty of ~ 0.2 "
