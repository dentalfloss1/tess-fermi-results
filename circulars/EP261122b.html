GCN Circular 46335: EP261122b (WXT trigger 01709322835): NOT Optical Candidate Counterpart

Event ID: EP261122b
Submitter: Gregory Corcoran at University College Dublin <gregory.corcoran@ucdconnect.ie>
Created: 2026-11-22 21:30:02 UTC
Source file: 46335.json

G. Corcoran (UCD), D. B. Malesani (DAWN/NBI and Radboud), P. G. Jonker (Radboud), A. Martin-Carrillo (UCD), J. Chacón (PUC), O. Stalder (NOT), M. R. Alexandersen (NOT) report on behalf of a larger collaboration:

We observed the field of the Einstein Probe WXT trigger 01709322835 (likely named EP261122b) using the ALFOSC camera on the Nordic Optical Telescope (NOT). Observations consisted of 9 x 200 s exposures in the SDSS z-band and started at 19:14:19 UT on 2026-11-22 (midtime: 10.50 hours post trigger).

In our stacked image we identify a source not seen in Legacy Survey at the coordinates (J2000):

R.A. : 01:11:12.17
Dec. : +01:48:01.9

For this source we measure a magnitude of 

z = 21.94 +/- 0.17,

measured in the AB system, calibrated against nearby stars from the Pan-STARRS catalog, and not corrected for Galactic extinction.

GCN Circular 46343: EP261122b: Einstein Probe detection of an X-ray transient

Event ID: EP261122b
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-23 03:28:16 UTC
Source file: 46343.json

G.J. Yang (NAOC, CAS), S.Q. Zheng, J.R. Liu, Y.J. Yi(BNU), H.W. Peng(THU) and H.W. Pan (NAOC, CAS) on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:

We report on the detection of an X-ray transient by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission, designated EP261122b. The transient triggered EP-WXT (ID: 01709322835) at 2026-11-22T09:01:06 (UTC). Subsequent follow-up observations were reported by Corcoran et al. (GCN 46335). The WXT position of the source is R.A. = 17.789 deg, DEC = 1.794 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 3 arcmin in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic).

A follow-up observation with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) was performed automatically. Within the WXT error circle, an uncatalogued X-ray source was detected at R.A. = 17.8020 deg, DEC = 1.8008 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 10 arcsec in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic). The FXT position is consistent with that reported by Corcoran et al. (GCN 46335) within the 90% error radius.

Further information will be updated when the telemetry data is received.

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

GCN Circular 46356: EP261122b: refined analysis of the EP-WXT and EP-FXT observations

Event ID: EP261122b
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-24 12:50:59 UTC
Source file: 46356.json

G.J. Yang (NAOC, CAS), S.Q. Zheng, J.R. Liu, Y.J. Yi(BNU), H.W. Peng(THU) and H.W. Pan (NAOC, CAS) on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:

The fast X-ray transient EP261122b triggered the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission at 2026-11-22T09:01:06 (UTC) (Yang et al., GCN 46343). Subsequent follow-up observations were reported by Corcoran et al. (GCN 46335). The refined analysis of the WXT data shows that the event started at T0 = 2026-11-22T08:59:04 (UTC) and lasted for ~120 s with a single pulse, before the observation was interrupted by the autonomous follow-up observation.

The WXT spectrum extracted over the burst interval in the 0.5–4 keV band can be fitted by an absorbed power-law model with the hydrogen column density fixed at the Galactic value of 3.0×10^20 cm^-2. The photon index is 1.61 (+0.4/−0.44), and the corresponding unabsorbed 0.5-4 keV flux is 6.1 (+2.1/-1.4)×10^-10 erg s^-1 cm^-2.

The autonomous observation by the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) was performed at 2026-11-22 09:05:03 (UTC), about 6 minutes after T0. An uncatalogued X-ray source was detected at R.A. = 17.8019 deg, Dec. = 1.8008 deg (J2000), with an uncertainty of 10 arcsec in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic). This position is consistent with the optical source reported by Corcoran et al. (GCN 46335). 

The average FXT 0.5-10 keV spectrum can be fitted with an absorbed power-law model. With the column density fixed at the Galactic value of 3.0×10^20 cm^-2. The photon index is 2.99 (+0.16/-0.16), and the derived average unabsorbed 0.5-10 keV flux is 1.20 (+0.08/-0.07)×10^-12 erg s^-1 cm^-2. The uncertainties are at the 1 sigma confidence level for the above spectral parameters.

Further multi-wavelength follow-up observations are encouraged.

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).
