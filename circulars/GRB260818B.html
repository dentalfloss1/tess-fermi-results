GCN Circular 45398: GRB 260818B: SVOM detection of a short burst

Bertrand Cordier (CEA), Hatsune Goto (RIKEN, CEA) report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:

At 2026-08-18T20:11:12 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 260818B (SVOM burst-id sb26081807).

The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was only detected by the Count-Rate Trigger (CRT), which produced a sequence of 2 alerts. CRT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 18.60 in the [8-120] keV energy band over a time window of 0.08 seconds starting at 2026-08-18T20:11:12.

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 44.9407, 7.4547 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 2h59m45.76s
Dec. (J2000) = 7d27m16.93s
 with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 4.60 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

The SVOM/ECLAIRs light curve showed a single peak structure with a duration of about 1 second.

This burst also triggered SVOM/GRM at 2026-08-18T20:11:12 on a timescale of 0.10 seconds with an SNR of 31.10.

Due to a platform constraint, no immediate slew was performed on this burst.
No X-ray observation could be performed by SVOM/MXT for the time being. No optical observation could be performed by SVOM/VT for the time being.

We will schedule a SVOM ToO observation for this burst.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. SVOM/ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC. SVOM/GRM was developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS. SVOM/MXT was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IJCLab, University of Leicester, MPE.

GCN Circular 45405: GRB 260818B: SVOM/ECLAIRs refined analysis

H. Yang, O. Godet, S. Guillot (IRAP),  Bertrand Cordier, S. Schanne (CEA), Hatsune Goto (RIKEN, CEA) 
report on behalf of the SVOM/ECLAIRs team: 

Using the event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station, we report further analysis of SVOM/ECLAIRs observations of the short burst GRB 260818B (SVOM burst-id sb26081807, GCN 45398, trigger time T0 = 2026-08-18T20:11:12.618 UTC). 

The burst that triggered ECLAIRs shows a typical fast-rise, exponential-decay (FRED) light curve in the 4-120 keV band, with T90(4-120 keV) = (0.22 +0.07/-0.02) s. The light curve in the 4-20 keV band shows possible extended emission following the main pulse, with a corresponding T90(4-20 keV) = (0.71 +0.40/-0.39) s. 

The time-averaged spectrum of the main pulse, extracted from T0 to T0 + 0.22 s in the 5-120 keV range, is well fitted by a cutoff power-law model with a photon index of -0.61 +/-0.14, a peak energy of (56 +16/-12) keV, a 4-120 keV photon flux of (32.3 +0.1/-4.8) photons/cm^2/s, and a corresponding 4-120 keV fluence of (2.9 +/-0.2)e-7 erg/cm^2. 

The time-averaged spectrum of the possible extended emission, extracted from T0 + 0.22 s to T0 + 0.71 s in the 5-20 keV, can be well fitted by a blackbody model with a temperature of (1.6 +0.4/-0.3) keV, a 4-120 keV photon flux of (2.1 +0.3/-0.7) photons/cm^2/s, and a corresponding 4-120 keV fluence of (1.1+/-0.3) e-8 erg/cm^2.

All the quoted errors are at the 68% confidence level.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC.

  Dec(J2000) = 07:25:28.24 = 7.424511 degrees

First epoch, 2026-08-19, 13.20 hr after the trigger:
  r = 24.24 +/- 0.15 mag
  z > 23.81 mag (3-sigma upper limit)

Second epoch, 2026-08-25, 157.38 h after the trigger:
  r = 24.60 +/- 0.29 mag
  z = 22.84 +/- 0.15 mag

H. L. Li, L. P. Xin, C. Wu, Y. L. Qiu, Y. N. Ma, Z. H. Yao, J. R. Xu, J. Wang, X. H. Han, Y. Xu, P. P. Zhang, W. J. Xie, Y. J. Xiao, H. B. Cai, J. S. Deng, J. Y. Wei (NAOC), J. T. Palmerio, Bertrand Cordier (CEA/Irfu) report on behalf of the SVOM/VT team.

SVOM/VT performed several ToO observations for GRB 260818B, which was triggered by SVOM/ECLAIRs (sb26081807, Cordier et al., GCN 45398; Yang et al., GCN 45405). This burst was also detected by SVOM/GRM (Ren et al., GCN 45411), HXMT (Wang et al., GCN 45408). The earliest observation started at 2026-08-18T23:33:18 UTC, about 3.4 hr post trigger in the VT_B (400-650 nm) and VT_R (650-1000 nm) channels simultaneously. Additional ToO observations were performed at 1.02 d and 7.5 d post trigger.

No credible candidate was found in the stacked images for each epoch, within the SVOM/ECLAIRs error box (Cordier et al., GCN 45398), the VLA radio sources (Schroeder et al., GCN 45418) and the Einstein Probe X-ray sources (Wu et al., GCN 45407). The typical 5-sigma upper limits are 23.0 mag in both channels. Our measurements are in AB magnitudes and are not corrected for Galactic extinction. This non-detection is consitent with the report from LCO (Shi et al., GCN 45399), SVOM/COLIBRÍ (Guelfand et al., GCN 45401), NOT (O'Neill et al., GCN 45403) and SVOM-CGFT (Guan et al., GCN 45417).

We noticed that there seemed to be an optical brightening trend for the VLA radio source S10 (Schroeder et al., GCN 45418). Its brightness in VT_R was 22.2+/-0.3 mag at 5.1 hr to 21.7+/-0.2 mag at day 7. The position of S10 is spatially coincident with EPF_J025955.0+072535 reported by EP-FXT (Wu et al., GCN 45407).

In the vicinity of S10, an optical candidate was proposed by SVOM/COLIBRÍ (FM-GFT) (Ducoin et al., GCN 45450). Some contributions from the COLIBRÍ candidate may be blended into our photometry, since the angular separation between the two sources is only 1.7 arcsec. Given the limited spatial resolution of VT and the faintness of the candidate (Ducoin et al., GCN 45450), we cannot confirm its detection. But the light curve of S10 shows some positive trend which needs to be further confirmed.

GCN Circular 45458: GRB 260818B: VLT optical and nIR observations

Muskan Yadav, Massine El Kabir, Nicolás Monsalves, Hira Waseem, Yu-Han Yang and Eleonora Troja (U Rome) report on behalf of the ERC BHianca team:

We observed the field of short duration GRB 260818B detected by SVOM/ECLAIRs (Cordier et al., GCN 45398) with the FORS2 imager on the ESO VLT UT1 (Antu) and the HAWKI imager on the ESO VLT UT4 (Yepun). Both observations began 9.5 days after the trigger and were carried out at an average airmass ~1.2 and with an average seeing ~0.8 arcsec.

At the position of the candidate optical counterpart reported by COLIBRÍ (Ducoin et al., GCN 45450) we detect a faint (~24.7 AB) source in the z-band, but not in the J-band down to a 3-sigma limit >24.2 AB mag. Magnitudes are calibrated using nearby stars in the Pan-STARRS1 DR1 catalog (Chambers et al. 2016) and the 2MASS catalog (Skrutskie et al. 2006). Reported magnitudes are not corrected for Galactic extinction.

Our observations show that the optical source faded significantly between the two epochs, by at least ~2 mag between 6.5 d and 9.5 d after the burst. This would correspond to a power-law decay slope of ~-4.6, steeper than expected for standard GRB afterglows. 
The lack of a nIR detection suggests that its red color (r-z~1.8; Ducoin et al., GCN 45450) could be due to dust along the line of sight or, alternatively, that the late observations are dominated by the host galaxy light. 

Further observations are planned.

We thank the staff at the VLT for the rapid execution of these observations.
