GCN Circular 46267: GRB 261114A: SVOM detection of a burst

Event ID: GRB 261114A
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-14 10:14:00 UTC
Source file: 46267.json

An Li (BNU), Hatsune Goto (CEA/RIKEN), Nicolas Dagoneau (CEA), Diego Götz (CEA), Andrea Saccardi (CEA), Bertrand Cordier (CEA),  Ziqi Wang (GXU), Wenjin Xie (NAOC)  report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:

At 2026-11-14T09:33:13 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 261114A (SVOM burst-id sb26111401). The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was detected by the Image Trigger (IMT), from which we received a sequence of 4 alerts. At the time of reception, IMT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 13.84 in the [8-120] keV energy band over a time window of 40.96 seconds starting at 2026-11-14T09:32:53. Note that at least 25 alerts were generated but only 4 have been received.

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 345.8973, -32.3702 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 23h03m35.35s
Dec. (J2000) = -32d22m12.60s
 with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 5.91 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

The SVOM/ECLAIRs light curve showed a single broad peak structure with a T90 duration of about 46 -9 / +6 s.

SVOM slewed to the burst. SVOM/MXT began observing the field after the slew. Using onboard processed data, no X-ray source was detected with MXT.
VT began observing the field after the slew. The analysis of the data will be published in a future circular.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. SVOM/ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC. SVOM/GRM was developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS. SVOM/MXT was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IJCLab, University of Leicester, MPE.

GCN Circular 46290: GRB 261114A: VLT/FORS2 spectroscopic redshift z = 3.716

Event ID: GRB 261114A
Submitter: Andrea Saccardi at CEA/Irfu <andrea.saccardi@cea.fr>
Created: 2026-11-15 10:23:11 UTC
Source file: 46290.json

A. Saccardi (CEA/Irfu), J. An (NAOC), G. Corcoran (UCD), D. B. Malesani (DAWN/NBI and Radboud), E. Le Floc’h (CEA/Irfu), H. Fausey (Baylor), S. D. Vergani (Observatoire de Paris/LUX) report on behalf of the Stargate collaboration:

We observed the optical counterpart (Wu et al., GCN 46269; Zhao et al., GCN 46272; Li et al., GCN 46273; Li et al., GCN 46277; Schneider et al., GCN 46278) of the SVOM/ECLAIRs GRB 261114A (Li et. al., GCN 46267) using the ESO VLT UT1 (Antu) equipped with the FORS2 spectrograph.

In a 60 s I-band exposure taken with the acquisition camera (18.07 hr after the SVOM trigger), the counterpart is detected with a magnitude i = 22.6 +/- 0.2 (AB), calibrated against nearby objects from the SkyMapper catalog, and not corrected for Galactic extinction.

Our observation started on 2026 November 15 at 03:44:31 UT (18.19 hr after the SVOM trigger). A series of 3 spectra of 960 s each were obtained with the 600RI grism, covering the wavelength range 5300-8450 AA.

In a preliminary reduction of the spectra, we clearly detect a continuum over the entire wavelength range. A trough is visible at around ~5730 AA, which we interpret as due to Lyman alpha at z ~ 3.7. From the detection of multiple narrow absorption features, including Si II, Si IV, and C IV, we infer a redshift of z = 3.716.

We acknowledge excellent support from the ESO staff in Paranal.

GCN Circular 46306: GRB 261114A: SVOM/ECLAIRs refined analysis

Event ID: GRB 261114A
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-16 15:05:43 UTC
Source file: 46306.json

Authors: N. Dagoneau (CEA), M. Brunet (IRAP), A. Li (BNU), H. Goto (CEA/RIKEN) report on behalf of the SVOM/ECLAIRs team

Using the event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station, we report further analysis of SVOM/ECLAIRs observations of GRB 261114A (SVOM burst-id sb26111401 - GCN 46267, trigger time T0 = 2026-11-14T09:33:13 UTC). 

The burst that triggered ECLAIRs onboard shows a broad multiple peak structure. The burst duration, determined through imaging, is T90 = 73.9 -9.3/+7.8 s in the 4-120 keV energy band.

The time-averaged spectrum from T0-17.68 s to T0+56.70 s in the 5-120 keV energy range is best fitted by a power law model with a power-law index alpha = -1.58 ± 0.07 . With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is (2.14 ± 0.20)e-6 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is (9.31 ± 0.43)e-1 ph/cm^2/s. 

All the quoted errors are at the 68% confidence level.

GCN Circular 46308: Konus-Wind detection of GRB 261114A

Event ID: GRB 261114A
Submitter: Anna Ridnaia at Ioffe Institute <ridnaia@mail.ioffe.ru>
Created: 2026-11-16 17:53:58 UTC
Source file: 46308.json

A. Ridnaia, D. Frederiks, A. Lysenko, V. Panteleeva,
D. Svinkin, A. Tsvetkova,  M. Ulanov, and T. Cline,
on behalf of the Konus-Wind team, report:

The long-duration GRB 261114A
(SVOM detection: Li et al., GCN 46267; 
Dagoneau et al., GCN 46306)
was detected by Konus-Wind (KW) in the waiting mode
at about T0 = T0 (SVOM) = 34393 s UT (09:33:13).

A Bayesian block analysis of the KW  waiting mode data
in the 20-400 keV band reveals a ~7 sigma count rate
increase in the interval from T0+11.011 s to T0+22.787 s.

The Konus-Wind light curve of this GRB is available at
http://www.ioffe.ru/LEA/GRBs/GRB261114A/

The total burst fluence is 1.64(-0.44,+0.62)x10^-6 erg/cm^2,
and the 2.944 s peak energy flux, measured from T0+11.011 s,
is 1.61(-0.55,+0.71)x10^-7 erg/cm^2/s.
(both in the 20 keV - 10 MeV energy range).

The time-integrated spectrum of the burst,
measured from T0+11.011 s to T0+22.787 s,
can be described by a simple power law with 
the photon index of -2.22 (-0.24,+0.19),
chi2 = 1.1/1 dof.

Assuming the redshift z=3.716 (Saccardi et al., GCN 46290)
and a standard cosmology with H_0 = 67.3 km/s/Mpc, Omega_M = 0.315,
and Omega_Lambda = 0.685 (Planck Collaboration, 2014),
we estimate the following rest-frame parameters:
the isotropic energy release E_iso is 4.70(-1.25,+1.79)x10^52 erg,
and the peak luminosity L_iso is 2.18(-0.75,+0.95)x10^52 erg/s.

All the quoted errors are estimated at the 68% confidence level.
All the presented results are preliminary.

GCN Circular 46310: GRB 261114A: VST/OmegaCAM upper limits at 1.6 days

Event ID: GRB 261114A
Submitter: Cheng Cheng at CAS South America Center for Astronomy (CASSACA) <chengcheng@nao.cas.cn>
Created: 2026-11-17 01:55:05 UTC
Source file: 46310.json

Cheng Cheng (CASSACA, NAOC), Liping Xin (NAOC), Jia-Sheng Huang (CASSACA, NAOC) report:

We observed the field of GRB 261114A (SVOM/ECLAIRs, GCN 46267; optical counterpart （Wu et al., GCN 46269; Zhao et al., GCN 46272; Li et al., GCN 46273; Li et al., GCN 46277; Schneider et al., GCN 46278，Saccardi et al., GCN 46290) with the ESO VLT Survey Telescope (VST) equipped with OmegaCAM, under CNTAC proposal ID CN2026B-62 (PI: Jia-Sheng Huang) including VST project, ESO programme 117.2B2A.001. Three 300-s exposures were obtained in each of the SDSS g and r filters starting at 2026-11-16 00:34:57 UT, i.e. T0 + 39.03 hr, where T0 = 2026-11-14 09:33:13 UT is the SVOM/ECLAIRs trigger time (the g frames were taken at T0 + 39.03-39.22 hr and the r frames at T0 + 39.33-39.52 hr). The observations were carried out at an airmass of 1.02-1.04, with the Moon 36% illuminated at an angular distance of 34.4 deg, and the FWHM of point sources in our images is 0.8-1.1 arcsec.

The optical afterglow is located at RA, Dec = 23:03:15.21, -32:19:01.2 (J2000). It is **not detected** in our stacked images.

Photometry is calibrated against Legacy Survey DR11 DECam photometry of stars in the same CCD, transformed to the SDSS system following Dey et al. (2019). The 3-sigma limiting magnitudes for a point source in a 1-arcsec-diameter aperture are:

Filter   T-T0 (hr)   3-sigma limit (AB), 1-arcsec aperture
  r        39.43        24.1
  g        39.13        24.5

We note that this burst is at z = 3.716 (GCN 46290), for which Lyman-alpha falls at 5734 A, i.e. inside the SDSS r band, while the SDSS g band lies entirely blueward of the break. The g-band non-detection therefore carries little information on the afterglow brightness.

Our limits are consistent with an extrapolation of the SVOM/VT light curve (GCN 46277) using the post-break decay measured at 12-55 ks (F ~ t^-1.2), which predicts r ~ 24.0 at the epoch of our observations, essentially at our 3-sigma limit.

The quoted limits are not corrected for Galactic extinction (E(B-V) = 0.020 along this line of sight).

We thank the ESO Paranal staff and the VST team for the prompt execution of these target-of-opportunity observations.
