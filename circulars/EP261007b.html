GCN Circular 45847: EP261007b: Einstein Probe detection of an X-ray transient

Event ID: EP261007b
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-10-08 03:02:37 UTC
Source file: 45847.json

W.F. Wen (SZTU), L.N. Xu (USTC), H.Y. Liu (NAO, CAS), Q.H. Lao (NAO, CAS), X.H. Tang (THU) and C.C. Jin (NAO, CAS) on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:

We report on the detection of an X-ray transient by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission, designated EP261007b. The transient triggered EP-WXT (ID: 01709321008) at 2026-10-07T15:05:16(UTC). The WXT position of the source is R.A. = 356.349 deg, DEC = 11.369 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 3 arcmin in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic).
 
A follow-up observation with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) was performed automatically. Within the WXT error circle, an uncatalogued X-ray source was detected at R.A. = 356.3481 deg, DEC = 11.3539 deg (J2000) with an uncertainty of 20 arcsec in radius (90% C.L. statistical and systematic).
Further information will be updated when the telemetry data are received.

Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space X-ray observatory to monitor the soft X-ray sky with X-ray follow-up capability (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

We observed the field of EP261007b detected by EP/WXT (Wen et al., GCN 45847) with LATIOS on SVOM/C-GFT. Our observations started at 2026-10-07T15:23:10 UTC, ~ 17.9 min after the trigger.

i > 20.4 AB (3-sigma, mid-time 0.59 hr after the trigger).

J. A. Quirola-Vasquez (Radboud), A. Martin-Carrillo (UCD), D. B. Malesani (DAWN/NBI and Radboud), G. Corcoran (UCD), R. A. J. Eyles-Ferris (Leicester), P. G. Jonker (Radboud), A. J. Levan (Radboud and Warwick), J. Chacon (PUC), and F. E. Bauer (SSI and UTA) report on behalf of a larger collaboration:

We observed the field of the X-ray transient EP261007b (Wen et al., GCN 45847) using the ALFOSC camera on the Nordic Optical Telescope (NOT). Observations consisted of a sequence of 5 x 300 s exposures in the SDSS r-band, and started on 2026-10-08 at 01:57:25 UT (~10.9 hr after the EP/WXT trigger time; Wen et al., GCN 45847).

After performing image subtraction on our stacked image against the Legacy Survey, no residuals are found within the EP-FXT error circle, although we note that the bright nearby star Gaia DR3 2763566336899590528 leaves some subtraction imperfections, affecting a small part of the EP-FXT area. We obtain an upper limit in our difference image within the EP-FXT region of r > 23.8 (AB), calibrated using nearby stars from the Pan-STARRS catalogue, and not corrected for Galactic extinction.

We do however note the presence of a transient source outside (51" away) of the FXT region at coordinates (J2000) RA = 23:45:22.41, Dec= +11:20:25.8. For this source we measure a difference magnitude of r = 22.09 +/- 0.10. A faint object can be seen in the Legacy Survey at this position with r = 23.62 +/- 0.20 (AB). Given the low S/N of this archival detection, no morphological properties can be reliably inferred.


It is not expected for the FXT localization to be so far off the actual transient position, so the association between this object and EP261007b cannot be confirmed at the present time, but we encourage further monitoring as this source remains consistent with the EP/WXT localization.

GCN Circular 45872: EP261007b: Refined analysis of the EP-WXT and autonomous EP-FXT observations

Event ID: EP261007b
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-10-09 11:18:00 UTC
Source file: 45872.json

W.F. Wen (SZTU), Q.H. Lao (NAO, CAS), L.N. Xu (USTC), H.Y. Liu (NAO, CAS), X.H. Tang (THU), and C.C. Jin (NAO, CAS) report on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:

The X-ray transient EP261007b triggered the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission at 2026-10-07T15:05:16
 (Wen et al., GCN 45847). Subsequent follow-up observations were reported 
by Ma et al. (GCN 45849), Zhang et al. (GCN 45860), Mohan et al. (GCN 45861), Shi et al. (GCN 45862), Quirola-Vasquez et al. (GCN 45864), and Li et al. (GCN 45870). Here we present the analysis of the EP-WXT and autonomous EP-FXT observations of EP261007b.
The time-averaged WXT spectrum in the 0.5–4 keV band was fitted with an absorbed power-law model. The hydrogen column density was fixed at the Galactic value of NH = 3.59 × 10^20 cm^-2 at the source position. The best-fit photon index is 1.9 (+0.6/-0.4). The average unabsorbed 0.5–4 keV flux is 7.9 (+2.8/-2.3) × 10^-11 erg s^-1 cm^-2.
The Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) on board EP began its autonomous observation at 2026-10-07T15:10:24 UTC, 308 s after the WXT trigger. FXT-A detected two X-ray sources within the WXT error region reported in GCN 45847:
EPF_J234524.0+112110: R.A. = 356.3500 deg, Dec. = 11.3526 deg (J2000).
EPF_J234521.3+112424: R.A. = 356.3388 deg, Dec. = 11.4067 deg (J2000).
Each position has a 90% confidence error radius of approximately 10 arcsec, including statistical and systematic uncertainties.
For EPF_J234524.0+112110, we jointly fitted the FXT-A and FXT-B spectra in the 0.5–10 keV band with an absorbed power-law model. The hydrogen column density was fixed at the Galactic value. The best-fit photon index is 2.0 ± 0.4. The average unabsorbed 0.5–10 keV flux is 3.5 (+1.3/-0.8) × 10^-13 erg s^-1 cm^-2.
For EPF_J234521.3+112424, we fitted the FXT-B spectrum in the 0.5–10 keV band with an absorbed power-law model, fixing NH at the Galactic value. The best-fit photon index is 1.8 (+1.3/-1.0). The average unabsorbed 0.5–10 keV flux is 6.3 (+12.4/-3.8) × 10^-14 erg s^-1 cm^-2.
We note that a variable source was detected outside the FXT error circle by NOT (QuirolaVasquez et al., GCN 45864) and SVOM/VT (Li et al., GCN 45870). We have examined the FXT images and found no Xray source present at this position. More follow-up observations are encouraged. 
All quoted uncertainties on the spectral parameters and fluxes are at the 90% confidence level.
Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space-based X-ray observatory designed to monitor the soft X-ray sky and perform X-ray follow-up observations (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).

GCN Circular 45876: EP261007b: SVOM/ECLAIRs Upper Limit

H. Yang, O. Godet (IRAP)
report on behalf of the SVOM team: 

We performed a targeted search to the X-ray transient EP261007b, detected by EP-WXT at 2026-10-07T15:05:16 UTC (T0) (EP team, GCN 45847), with the SVOM/ECLAIRs event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station. 

At T0, the transient position was within the ECLAIRs field of view. No statistically significant signal was identified in the [T0 - 3000 s, T0 + 1000 s] interval by the ECLAIRs offline trigger. 

For the interval from T0 to T0 + 16 s, we derive a 3-sigma upper limit of 1.2e-8 erg/cm^2/s in the 4-120 keV energy band, assuming a power-law spectrum with a photon index of -1.9, as measured by EP-FXT for the uncatalogued source (GCN 45872). Alternatively, assuming a “soft” spectral template (a Band function with Epeak = 25 keV, alpha = -1.5, beta = -3.0), we derive a 3-sigma upper limit of 1.1e-8 erg/cm^2/s in the 4-120 keV energy band. We note that the EP/WXT flux of 7.9e-11 erg/cm^2/s in the 0.5-4 keV (GCN 45872) is more than two orders of magnitude lower than our 3-sigma upper limit.

GCN Circular 45902: EP261007b: Second EP-FXT follow-up observation and a possible X-ray counterpart to the optical transient

Event ID: EP261007b
Submitter: EP Team at NAOC/CAS <ep_ta@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-10-11 15:56:45 UTC
Source file: 45902.json

W.F. Wen (SZTU), Q.H. Lao (NAO, CAS), L.N. Xu (USTC), H.Y. Liu (NAO, CAS), X.H. Tang (THU), and C.C. Jin (NAO, CAS) report on behalf of the Einstein Probe (EP) team:
The X-ray transient EP261007b was detected by the Wide-field X-ray Telescope (WXT) on board the Einstein Probe (EP) mission on 2026-10-07 (Wen et al., GCN 45847). A refined analysis of the EP-WXT and autonomous EP-FXT observations was subsequently reported by Wen et al. (GCN 45872).  Optical and near-infrared follow-up observations were reported by Ma et al. (GCN 45849), Zhang et al. (GCN 45860), Mohan et al. (GCN 45861), Shi et al. (GCN 45862), Quirola-Vasquez et al. (GCN 45864), and Li et al. (GCN 45870).
The EP-FXT performed a second follow-up observation of EP261007b beginning at 2026-10-10T04:43:36 (UTC), with an exposure of about 3566 s. No significant X-ray source was detected at the position of EP261007b. We derived a 3σ upper limit on its 0.5–10 keV flux of 2.3 × 10^-13 erg cm^-2 s^-1. This result indicates that the X-ray emission had faded since the initial EP observation.
Quirola-Vasquez et al. (GCN 45864) reported an optical transient outside the original FXT error circle. The transient was subsequently confirmed in archival SVOM/VT observations by Li et al. (GCN 45870). In the second FXT observation, an X-ray source was detected at R.A. = 356.3435 deg, Decl. = 11.3440 deg (J2000), with an offset of 12.6 arcsec from the optical position. The 0.5–10 keV spectrum from FXT-B is reasonably well fitted by an absorbed power-law model with the hydrogen column density fixed at the Galactic value. The best-fit photon index is 3.1 (+2.5/-2.2), corresponding to an unabsorbed 0.5–10 keV flux of 6.27 × 10^-14 erg cm^-2 s^-1.
Given its close proximity, this X-ray source might be associated with the optical source reported in GCN 45864 and GCN 45870, although additional observations are needed to firmly establish the connection.
We encourage further multiwavelength observations of EP261007b and the possible X-ray counterpart to the optical transient.
Launched on January 9, 2024, EP is a space-based X-ray observatory designed to monitor the soft X-ray sky and perform X-ray follow-up observations (Yuan et al. 2022, Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics).
