GCN Circular 46030: GRB 261026A: SVOM detection of a burst

Event ID: GRB 261026A
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-10-26 06:36:32 UTC
Source file: 46030.json

Hatsune Goto (RIKEN/CEA), Clara Plasse (HKU), Wenjin Xie, Chao WU (NAOC), Chenwei Wang (IHEP) report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:

At 2026-10-26T06:09:37 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 261026A (SVOM burst-id sb26102601).

The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was detected both by the Count-Rate Trigger (CRT) and the Image Trigger (IMT), which produced a sequence of 8 alerts. CRT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 11.10 in the [5-20] keV energy band over a time window of 20.40 seconds starting at 2026-10-26T06:09:27.

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 337.3314, 27.0485 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 22h29m19.52s
Dec. (J2000) = 27d02m54.66s
 with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 7.22 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

This burst was also detected by SVOM/GRM with a significance of 11.20.
SVOM slewed to the burst.

SVOM/MXT and VT began observing the field after the slew. The analysis of the data will be published in future circulars.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. SVOM/ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC. SVOM/GRM was developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS. SVOM/MXT was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IJCLab, University of Leicester, MPE.

The Burst Advocate (BA) on shift for this alert is Chao WU: cwu@nao.cas.cn. Please contact the BA by email if you require additional information.

WeiKang Zheng (UCB), Yun Wang (UCB), Xuhui Han (NAOC),

Pinpin Zhang (NAOC), Yun Wang (UCB) and Alexei V. Filippenko (UCB)

report on behalf of the KAIT GRB team:


The 0.76-m Katzman Automatic Imaging Telescope (KAIT), located at

Lick Observatory, observed the field of SVOM GRB 261026A (Goto

et al., GCN 46030) starting at 06:11:44 UT, 127 seconds after SVOM

trigger. Observations were performed in 3 x 3 tiling mode, and a

set of clear (roughly R) filter images were obtained. In one of

the fields, we detected the uncatalogued source reported by Dornic

et al. (GCN 46033) with a mag of 18.1 +/- 0.1 mag at 290 seconds

after the burst. The object continued fading in our follow up

observations, we therefore confirm it to be the optical counterpart

of GRB 261026A. Observations are on going.

Yun Shi, Jin-Ji Li, Shu-Quan Bai, Jian-Hong Cao, Zhong-Nan Dong, Rui-Chen Gao, Si-Rui Huang, Jia-Qi Lin, Pu Lin, Jing-Kai Sun, Zhi-Xiang Tan, Lu-Yao Wen, Hao-Nan Yang, Hao-Ran Zhang, P. H. Thomas Tam, Rong-Feng Shen, and Bin Ma (Sun Yat-sen University) report on behalf of the SYSU 80cm infrared telescope team: 

We observed the field of GRB 261026A, detected by SVOM/ECLAIRs (Goto et al., GCN 46030), using the Sun Yat-sen University (SYSU) 80 cm infrared telescope in the J band. Our observations were carried out on 2026 Oct 26 from 11:50:00 to 12:50:00 UT (from ~5.67 to ~6.67 hours after the SVOM/ECLAIRs trigger), using stacked images in the J band (165 x 20 s, 3300 s total exposure).

We clearly detect the near-infrared afterglow at the position of the previously reported optical counterpart (Dornic et al., GCN 46033; Zheng et al., GCN 46034; Mohan et al., GCN 46041; Li et al., GCN 46046), and we measure a magnitude of:
J = 19.37 +/- 0.14 mag (Vega), at a mid-time of ~6.17 hours post-trigger.

Assuming a standard J-band conversion of J (AB) = J (Vega) + 0.91, our measurement corresponds to J ~ 20.28 mag (AB). Comparing this with the early J-band detection by WINTER (J = 18.55 +/- 0.1 mag, AB, at T0+12.6 min; Mohan et al., GCN 46041), our result indicates a significant fading of ~1.73 mag over ~6 hours. 

The photometry was calibrated against nearby stars from the 2MASS catalog and is reported in the Vega system, without correction for Galactic extinction.

The SYSU 80 cm infrared telescope is operated and managed by the Department of Astronomy, Sun Yat-sen University.

We report on observations of the optical counterpart of GRB 261026A detected by SVOM/ECLAIRs and SVOM/GRM (Goto et al., GCN 46030).

We observed the field of GRB 261026A with a Las Cumbres Observatory (LCO) 40-cm telescope located at the LCO node at the Haleakalā High Altitude Observatory (HO), Maui, Hawaii. The observation,a single 500-sec exposure in the SDSS i' filter, started on 2026-10-26 at 08:03:57 UT (about 1.906 hr after the SVOM trigger).

This result is consistent with other optical and near-infrared observations (Dornic et al., GCN 46033; Zheng et al., GCN 46034; Wu et al., GCN 46035; Mohan et al., GCN 46041; Li et al., GCN 46046; and Shi et al., GCN 46047).

Stella Manyan (Columbia U), K. De (Columbia U), G. Mo (Caltech/Carnegie), V. Karambelkar (Columbia U), D. Schiminovich (Columbia U) report:

We observed the field of GRB 261026A discovered by SVOM (Goto et al., GCN 46030), also detected by EP-FXT (Jiang et al., GCN 46058) in the near-infrared J and Hs band with the MDM InfraRed Astronomy inGaas Experiment (MIRAGE) instrument on the MDM 1.3m telescope.

A near-infrared counterpart was detected in J band by WINTER (J = 18.55 +/- 0.1 AB mag at T0+12.6 min; Mohan et al., GCN 46041), which faded to 20.28 mag over ~6 hours as reported by the SYSU 80cm infrared telescope (Shi et al., GCN 46047). Several optical detections have also been reported by other facilities (Zheng et al., GCN 46034; Morín-González et al., GCN 46048; Zhao et al., GCN 46056).

Observations began at 2026-10-27T01:31:05 (19.4 hours after SVOM trigger) in the J band lasting 1620 s, and 2026-10-27 02:06:03 (19.9 hours after trigger) in the Hs band lasting 2160 s. We detect a faint infrared source in Hs band at ~ 2.5 sigma significance at Hs =  20.484 ± 0.486 AB mag, while we obtain an upper limit in the J band with J > 19.83 AB mag.

MIRAGE is a new robotic YJHs-band near infrared imager for the MDM 1.3 m telescope. We thank the Mt Cuba astronomical foundation for supporting development of MIRAGE and MDM Observatory staff for supporting the observations

GRB 261026A: SVOM/ECLAIRs refined analysis

W. Xie (NAOC), N. Dagoneau (CEA), M. Brunet (IRAP), H. Goto (RIKEN/CEA), C. Plasse (HKU), C. Wu (NAOC), C. Wang (IHEP) report on behalf the SVOM/ECLAIRs team

Using the event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station, we report further analysis of SVOM/ECLAIRs observations of GRB 261026A (SVOM burst-id sb26102601 – GCN 46030, trigger time T0 = 2026-10-26T06:09:37.68 UTC). 

The burst that triggered ECLAIRs onboard shows a broad bump lightcurve. The burst duration is T90 = 26.7 +1.5/-1.6 s in the 4-120 keV energy band.

The time-averaged spectrum from T0-8.3 s to T0+18.4 s in the 4-120 keV energy range is well fitted by a powerlaw model  with alpha = -1.82 +/- 0.18. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is  (1.37 ± 0.18)e-6 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is 2.03 ± 0.13 ph/cm^2/s. 


All the quoted errors are at the 68% confidence level.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC.
