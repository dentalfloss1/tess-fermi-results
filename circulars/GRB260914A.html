C. Wang (IHEP), W. Xie (NAOC), N. Dagoneau (CEA), D. Götz (CEA), C. Plasse (HKU),  K. Liu (GXU), Y. Ma (NAOC), report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:

At 2026-09-14T11:12:31 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 260914A (SVOM burst-id sb26091401). The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was detected both by the Count-Rate Trigger (CRT) and the Image Trigger (IMT), which produced a sequence of 9 alerts. CRT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 12.81 in the [5-20] keV energy band over a time window of 10.20 seconds starting at 2026-09-14T11:12:27.

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 31.5419, 29.2616 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 2h06m10.06s
Dec. (J2000) = 29d15m41.72s
 with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 6.33 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

This burst also triggered SVOM/GRM at 2026-09-14T11:12:32 on a timescale of 1 seconds with an SNR of 9.30.
The SVOM/GRM light curve showed a single narrow peak structure with a T90 duration of about 4.327 (-0.558 +2.287). The lightcurve of GRM could be found in the link below: https://www.bursthub.cn/gcn/2026/svgrb260914A.png

SVOM slewed to the burst. MXT and VT began observing the field after the slew. The analysis of the data will be published in a future circular.

GCN Circular 45579: GRB 260914A: VLT/X-shooter spectroscopic redshift z = 0.778

Event ID: GRB 260914A
Submitter: Andrea Saccardi at CEA/Irfu <andrea.saccardi@cea.fr>
Created: 2026-09-15 08:47:39 UTC
Source file: 45579.json

A. Saccardi (CEA/Irfu), M. Garnichey (LUX-Paris Obs.), D. B. Malesani (DAWN/NBI and Radboud), G. Corcoran (UCD), V. D’Elia (ASI/SSDC), M. De Pasquale (U. of Messina), L. Izzo (INAF/OAC), A. L. Thakur (INAF/IAPS), S. D. Vergani (LUX-Paris Obs.), report on behalf of the Stargate collaboration:

We observed the optical counterpart (Akl et al., GCN 45564; Palmerio et al., GCN 45565; Li et 
al., GCN 45566; Shi et al., GCN 45567; Saccardi et al., GCN 45568) of the SVOM GRB 260914A (Wang et al., GCN 45562; see also Hamburg, GCN 45569) using the ESO VLT UT3 (Melipal) equipped with the X-shooter spectrograph.

In a 30-s image taken with the acquisition camera (20.189 hr after the SVOM/ECLAIRs trigger), the counterpart is detected with a magnitude r = 22.19 +- 0.12 AB, calibrated against Pan-STARRS and not corrected for Galactic extinction. This is similar to the magnitude r ~ 22.1 of the underlying object seen in the Legacy survey, indicating that the transient is no longer dominating the total emission.

A spectrum covering the wavelength range 3000-21000 AA was secured, with a total exposure time of 4x600 s in each of the UVB, VIS, and NIR arms. Our observation started at 07:33 UT on 2026 September 15, i.e. about 20.34 hr after the SVOM trigger.

From a preliminary analysis, only faint continuum is present, but clear emission lines are detected in the visible and near-infrared arms, which we identify as H-alpha, the [O III] 4959, 5007 doublet, H-beta, and the [O II] 3726, 3729 doublet, all at a common redshift of z = 0.778.

The chance association probability between the underlying archival object at z = 0.778 and the afterglow is around 0.3% (Bloom et al. 2001), indicating that this is the likely host galaxy. We note though the presence of a bright galaxy (LEDA 1860670) some 10" away from the counterpart, with a spectroscopic redshift z = 0.112 from DESI (DESI collaboration et al., doi:10.3847/1538-3881/ae4c43) and a larger, but comparable, chance association probability. As long GRBs have mostly small host offsets, we favor the z = 0.778 interpretation, though we cannot conclusively rule out an association with the low-redshift galaxy.


We acknowledge excellent support from the ESO staff in Paranal.

GCN Circular 45582: GRB 260914A: VLT/X-shooter observations and redshift confirmation

Event ID: GRB 260914A
Submitter: Yu-Han Yang at University of Rome Tor Vergata <yyang@roma2.infn.it>
Created: 2026-09-15 11:39:05 UTC
Source file: 45582.json

Y.-H. Yang, M. Yadav, E. Troja, N. Monsalves (U Rome) report on behalf of the ERC BHianca team:

We observed the field of GRB 260914A (Wang et al., GCN 45562) with the X-shooter spectrograph on the ESO Very Large Telescope (VLT). Observations began at UT 06:03:21 on 2026-09-15 (18.8 hours after the GRB trigger) at an average airmass of ~1.8, and consisted of spectroscopy (4 × 600 s) and imaging with the acquisition and guiding (A&G) camera (360 s in each of the r and z filters).

We placed the 1 arcsec slit center at the position of the optical counterpart (Akl et al., GCN 45564; Palmerio et al., GCN 45565; Li et al., GCN 45566; Shi et al., GCN 45567, Saccardi et al., GCN 45568).  Based on a preliminary analysis, we identify the emission lines of H-alpha, H-beta, [O II] and [O III] in the VIS and NIR arms confirming the presence of an underlying star-forming galaxy at z ~0.778, as reported by Saccardi et al. (GCN 45579). 

For this galaxy, the chance coincidence probability is Pcc~ 0.3% (Saccardi et al. GCN 45579). However, the burst is located ~10 arcsec from a bright (r~16.5 mag) and nearby (z~0.112) galaxy, LEDA 1860670, with Pcc~0.1% derived using the prescription of Becerra et al. (2023, MNRAS, 522, 5204) which is calibrated down to bright galaxies. Therefore, on probabilistic grounds, the host galaxy’s association remains uncertain. 

Finally, we note that the UVB arm shows a weak continuum. As the underlying galaxy has catalogued magnitude g~23.4 AB (Legacy Survey DR10, Dey et al. 2019), the observed trace is likely contributed from the GRB afterglow. Over the faint continuum, we tentatively identify weak Mg II absorptions at a consistent redshift of z ~0.778. If confirmed, this would solidify the association between the GRB and the underlying, distant galaxy. 


We thank the staff at the VLT for the rapid execution of these observations.

GCN Circular 45594: GRB 260914A: SVOM/ECLAIRs refined analysis

Event ID: GRB 260914A
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-09-16 13:26:51 UTC

M. Brunet (IRAP), N. Dagoneau (CEA), O. Godet (IRAP), C. Wang (IHEP), W. Xie (NAOC) report on behalf of the SVOM/ECLAIRs team:

Using the event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station, we report further analysis of SVOM/ECLAIRs observations of GRB 260914A (SVOM burst-id sb26091401 – GCN 45562, trigger time T0 = 2026-09-14T11:12:31 UTC), which was also detected by Fermi/GBM as a sub-threshold (Hamburg, GCN 45569).

The burst that triggered ECLAIRs onboard shows a main broad peak from T0-2.2 s to T0+8.3 s seen below 50 keV and a soft precursor from T0-10.4 s to T0-8.8 s seen below 20 keV. The burst duration estimated through the imaging technique is T90 =  15.5 -7.2/+1.5 s in the 4-120 keV energy band.

The time-averaged spectrum from T0-2.2 s to T0+8.3 s in the 5-120 keV energy range is best fitted by a powerlaw model, with alpha = -1.98 +/-0.07. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is (1.68 +/-0.11)e-6 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is 7.0 +/-0.3  ph/cm^2/s. 

Using a band model with alpha = -1 and beta = -2.5 (fixed) also gives a good fit with Epeak = 14.2 +2.0/-1.8 keV. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is (1.43 +/-0.08)e-6 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is 6.5 +/-0.3  ph/cm^2/s. 

Assuming a redshift of z=0.778 (Saccardi et al., GCN 45579, Yang et al., GCN 45582), a standard cosmology with H_0 = 67.3 km/s/Mpc and Omega_M = 0.315 (Planck Collaboration, 2014) and the previous band model with Epeak = 14.2 keV, we estimate the isotropic energy release E_iso is about 3.6 e51 erg. With these estimates, GRB 260914A appears close to the Ep,z and E_iso ranges distribution of Type II (collapsar-like) GRBs on the Amati relation reported by Willingale et al. 2017.

All the quoted errors are at the 68% confidence level.

Submitter: Jin-Ye Luo <jy_luo2002@163.com>
Created: 2026-09-16 16:14:48 UTC
Source file: 45596.json

SVOM/GRM team: Jin-Ye Luo, Wen-Jun Tan, Chen-Wei Wang, Yue Wang, Chao Zheng, Zheng-Hang Yu, Shi-Jie Zheng, Yue Huang, Shao-Lin Xiong, Shuang-Nan Zhang (IHEP)

SVOM/ECLAIRs team:

Report on behalf of the SVOM team:

SVOM/GRM was triggered in-flight by GRB 260914A at 2026-09-14T11:12:33.000 (T0), which is also detected by SVOM/ECLAIRs (Wang et al., GCN #45562; Brunet et al., GCN #45594) and Fermi/GBM (Fermi GBM team, GCN #45569). 

With the event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station, the GRM light curve shows that this burst consists of a single pulse with a possible precursor. The burst has a T90 of 3.5 +/-1.0 s in the 15-5000 keV band.

The SVOM/GRM light curve can be found here:
https://www.bursthub.cn/gcn/2026/svgrb260914A.png

With the localization from SVOM/ECLAIRs (GCN #45564), the time-averaged spectrum from T0-4 to T0+3 s is best fitted by a power-law function with an exponential high-energy cutoff. The power law index is -0.4 +0.7/-0.6 and the cutoff energy, parameterized as Epeak, is 33.3 +4.3/-5.2 keV. The event fluence (10-1000 keV) in this time interval is (6.30 +0.66/−0.63)E-07 erg/cm^2. 

The localization of GRB 260914A in the 'Amati' relation diagram is shown at: https://www.bursthub.cn/gcn/2026/svgrb260914A_amati.png

The Space Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA, China), National Center for Space Studies (CNES, France) and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS, China), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. GRM is developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS.

The SVOM/GRM point of contact for this burst is: Jin-Ye Luo (IHEP) (luojy@ihep.ac.cn)

GCN Circular 45605: GRB 260914A: 2.2m CAHA optical afterglow detection and 10.4m GTC redshift confirmation

Event ID: GRB 260914A
Submitter: Alberto Castro-Tirado at Inst.de Astro. de Andalucia <ajct@iaa.es>
Created: 2026-09-17 04:04:09 UTC
Source file: 45605.json

A.J. Castro-Tirado (IAA-CSIC), Y.-D. Hu (GXU), I. Perez-Garcia, M.D. Caballero-Garcia, M. Gritsevich, S. Guziy (IAA-CSIC), J. F. Agui Fernandez, M.M. Sanchez Andujar (CAHA), D. Slumstrup and D. Perez-Valladares (IAC, GRANTECAN), on behalf of a larger collaboration, report:

Following the detection of GRB 260914A by SVOM/ECLAIRs (Wang et al. GCN 45562), we triggered the 2.2m CAHA telescope (equipped with CAFOS) in Almeria (Spain). Images in the Sloan g, r, i and z bands were gathered on Sep 14 starting at 22:01 UT (i.e. ~10.85 hours after the trigger).

We still detect the optical afterglow in the gri-bands, confirming the fast decay based in all reports published so far (Akl et al., GCN 45564; Palmerio et al., GCN 45565; Li et al., GCN 45566; Shi et al., GCN 45567, Saccardi et al., GCN 45568, Volnova et al., GCN 45595).  On Sep 14, 22:28 UT, we measure  i:  21.1 ± 0.1  (on a 600s image coadd).

We also obtained optical spectroscopy at the 10.4m GTC telescope (equipped with OSIRIS+) in La Palma (Spain) on Sep 15, starting at 04:27 UT (i.e. ~17.25 hours after the trigger), covering the range 3800-9500 A. Based on a preliminary analysis, we identify emission lines of [O II] and H-beta, thus confirming the redshift of the underlying star-forming galaxy at z ~0.778, as reported by Saccardi et al. (GCN 45579) and Yan et al.  (GCN 45582).

Moreover, in the faint continuum extending to the bluest wavelengths in the R1000B grism (3x750s exposure), we tentatively identify the Mg II doublet in absorption at the redshift of z ~0.778, thus supporting other findings (Yan et al.  GCN 45582) and confirming the association between GRB 260914A and the underlying above-mentioned galaxy.

We thank the staff at both CAHA and GTC for their excellent support.
