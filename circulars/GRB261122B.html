GCN Circular 46336: GRB 261122B: SVOM detection of a burst

M. G. Bernardini (INAF-OAB, LUPM), S. Antier (IJCLAB), M. Dinatolo (INAF-OAB), Hatsune Goto (CEA/RIKEN) report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:
At 2026-11-22T22:46:55 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 261122B (SVOM burst-id sb26112202).

The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was detected both by the Count-Rate Trigger (CRT) and the Image Trigger (IMT), which produced a sequence of 24 alerts. CRT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 22.45 in the [8-120] keV energy band over a time window of 5.10 seconds starting at 2026-11-22T22:46:52.

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 3.9375, -38.8029 degrees (J2000) with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 3.97 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

This burst also triggered SVOM/GRM at 2026-11-22T22:46:54 on a timescale of 0.10 seconds with an SNR of 6.90.

SVOM slewed to the burst.

SVOM/MXT began observing the field at 2026-11-22T22:49:45 UTC, 170 seconds after T0. Using onboard processed data we found an uncatalogued X-ray source located at R.A., Dec. 3.9789, -38.8248 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 0h15m54.93s
Dec. (J2000) = -38d49m29.40s
with a 90% C.L. radius of 29.15 arcseconds.

This location is 2.34 arcminutes from the ECLAIRs onboard position. 
This position may be improved as more data is received.

VT began observing the field after the slew. The analysis of the data will be published in a future circular.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. SVOM/ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC. SVOM/GRM was developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS. SVOM/MXT was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IJCLab, University of Leicester, MPE.

GCN Circular 46365: GRB 261122B: SVOM/ECLAIRs refined analysis

Event ID: GRB 261122B
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-11-25 10:49:26 UTC

Authors: M. Dinatolo (INAF-OAB, University of Milan), M. G. Bernardini (INAF-OAB, LUPM), S. Antier (IJCLAB), S. Schanne (CEA/Irfu)  report on behalf of the SVOM/ECLAIRs team

Using the event-by-event data downloaded through the X-band ground station, we report further analysis of SVOM/ECLAIRs observations of GRB 261122B (SVOM burst-id sb26112202 - GCN 46336, trigger time T0 = 2026-11-22T22:46:55 UTC). 

The burst that triggered ECLAIRs onboard has duration T90 =222.8 -/+1.8 s in the 4-120 keV energy band. It shows a structure with 3 bright peaks.

The time-averaged spectrum from T0+2.7 s to T0+37 s (pre-slew) in the 5-120 keV energy range is best fitted by a power law model with a power-law index alpha = -1.70 +/- 0.12. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is (7.23 +/- 0.83)e-7 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is (7.37 +/- 0.58)e-1 ph/cm^2/s. 

The time-averaged spectrum from T0+156 s to T0+226 s (post-slew) in the 5-120 keV energy range is best fitted by a power law model with a power-law index alpha = -2.37 +/- 0.04. With this model, the 4-120 keV fluence is (1.70 +/- 0.05)e-6 erg/cm^2 and the 4-120 keV photon flux is (1.45 +/- 0.02) ph/cm^2/s. 

All the quoted errors are at the 68% confidence level.

**AUTHORS:** Cheng Cheng (CASSACA, NAOC), Liping Xin (NAOC), Jia-Sheng Huang (CASSACA, NAOC) report:

We observed the field of GRB 261122B (SVOM/ECLAIRs, GCN 46336; refined analysis: Dinatolo et al., GCN 46365; SVOM/GRM: Luo et al., GCN 46369; optical counterpart: Wu et al., GCN 46338; Corcoran et al., GCN 46340; Yin et al., GCN 46341; Globus et al., GCN 46342; Li et al., GCN 46344; Hellot et al., GCN 46348; 5-GHz radio counterpart: Gulati et al., GCN 46351) with the 4.1-m SOAR telescope (Goodman High Throughput Spectrograph, red camera, imaging mode), under CNTAC proposal ID CN2026B-62 (PI: Jia-Sheng Huang).

Three 300-s exposures were obtained in each of the SDSS g, r, i and z filters starting at 2026-11-26 02:48:26 UT, i.e. T0 + 76.03 hr, where T0 = 2026-11-22 22:46:55 UT is the SVOM/ECLAIRs trigger time.  The observations were carried out at an airmass of 1.13-1.34, with the Moon 96% illuminated at an angular distance of 101 deg, and the FWHM of point sources in our stacked images is 1.7-2.0 arcsec.

The optical afterglow is detected at

  RA, Dec = 00:15:53.72, -38:49:41.6 (J2000) = 3.973846, -38.828221 (deg)

with an uncertainty of 0.2", which is 0.16" from the SVOM/VT position (Wu et al., GCN 46338).

The frames were processed from the raw data with bias and flat-field correction and background subtraction, the astrometry was solved for each frame, and the aligned frames were median-combined onto a common grid of 0.289 arcsec/pixel and about 4.8' x 4.8'.  Photometry was carried out in a 2.0-arcsec-diameter aperture, and the aperture flux was corrected to total flux using the point-source PSF measured from field stars on the same images.  The magnitudes are calibrated against Legacy Survey DR11 photometry of stars in the field, and we obtain

  Filter  T-T0 (hr)   mag (AB)
  g       76.15       22.80 +/- 0.54 (phot) +/- 0.07 (cal)
  r       76.79       22.49 +/- 0.23 (phot) +/- 0.02 (cal)
  i       76.47       22.74 +/- 0.40 (phot) +/- 0.02 (cal)
  z       77.14       21.93 +/- 0.27 (phot) +/- 0.02 (cal)

where the first uncertainty is the photometric error and the second is the calibration (zeropoint) uncertainty.  The calibration uncertainty is largest in the g band, where only a few bright stars are available.

As the source is faint, we only performed simple aperture photometry; a more careful measurement would require smoothing the images and a more refined treatment.  No colour term has been applied between the SOAR and Legacy Survey filter systems; a colour term of about 0.1 mag would be appropriate for a refined calibration.

A source is listed in Legacy Survey DR11 at 0.31" from the afterglow position (ls_id_dr11 = 48423010763281420), with g = 25.37 +/- 0.19, r = 25.52 +/- 0.32 and i = 25.00 +/- 0.29 (AB), and no z-band detection.

The quoted magnitudes are not corrected for Galactic extinction (E(B-V) = 0.017 along this line of sight).

We thank the SOAR staff for the rapid execution of these observations.
