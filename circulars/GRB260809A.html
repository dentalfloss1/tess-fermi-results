GCN Circular 45331: GRB 260809A: SVOM detection of a faint burst

Event ID: GRB 260809A
Submitter: SVOM_group <svomgroup@bao.ac.cn>
Created: 2026-08-09 15:24:53 UTC
Source file: 45331.json

M. Brunet (IRAP), F. Piron (LUPM), D.Gotz (CEA), A. Saccardi (CEA) report on behalf of the SVOM mission team:

At 2026-08-09T14:57:33 UTC (T0), SVOM/ECLAIRs triggered and located the gamma-ray burst GRB 260809A (SVOM burst-id sb26080907).

The following trigger information was received on the ground with low latency by the SVOM VHF Alert Network.

The burst was only detected by the Image Trigger (IMT), which produced a sequence of 2 alerts. IMT provided the alert with the best signal-to-noise-ratio in the image (SNR) of 7.20 in the [8-120] keV energy band over a time window of 20.48 seconds starting at 2026-08-09T14:57:13.

The localization of the best alert is R.A., Dec. 356.820, 20.969 degrees (J2000) with a 90% confidence level (C.L.) radius of 10.88 arcmin (including systematic error of 2 arcmin added in quadrature).

SVOM slewed to the burst.

SVOM/MXT began observing the field at 2026-08-09T15:00:23 UTC, 170 seconds after T0. Using onboard processed data we found an uncatalogued X-ray source located at R.A., Dec. 356.9664, 21.0347 degrees:
R.A. (J2000) = 23h47m51s
Dec. (J2000) =21d02m04s
with a 90% C.L. radius of 94 arcseconds.

This location is 9.09 arcminutes from the ECLAIRs onboard position. This position may be improved as more data is received.

VT began observing the field after the slew. The analysis of the data will be published in a future circular.

The Space-based multi-band astronomical Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA), French Space Agency (CNES), and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. SVOM/ECLAIRs was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IRAP, CNRS-APC. SVOM/GRM was developed by the Institute of High Energy Physics (IHEP) of CAS. SVOM/MXT was developed jointly by CNES, CEA-IRFU, CNRS-IJCLab, University of Leicester, MPE.

The Burst Advocate (BA) on shift for this alert is Frédéric Piron: piron@in2p3.fr.

GCN Circular 45336: GRB 260809A: SVOM/VT optical counterpart

Submitter: Huali Li at at NAOC, SVOM <lhl@nao.cas.cn>
Created: 2026-08-09 22:39:24 UTC

H. L. Li, C. Wu, L. P. Xin, Y. L. Qiu, Y. N. Ma, Z. H. Yao, J. R. Xu, X. H. Han, J. Wang, Y. Xu, P. P. Zhang, W. J. Xie, Y. J. Xiao, H. B. Cai, J. S. Deng, J. Y. Wei (NAOC), J. T. Palmerio (CEA/Irfu), F. Piron (LUPM) report on behalf of the SVOM/VT team.

SVOM/VT performed observations with automatic slew for GRB 260809A, which was triggered by SVOM/ECLAIRs (sb26080907, Brunet et al., GCN 45331) and also detected by SVOM/MXT (Brunet et al., GCN 45331). The observation started at 2026-08-09T15:01:31 UTC, i.e., 238 seconds post trigger in the VT_B (400-650 nm) and VT_R (650-1000 nm) channels simultaneously.

An uncatalogued optical counterpart was detected within the error circles of MXT (Brunet et al., GCN 45331)and ECLAIRs (Brunet et al., GCN 45331), compared with the Legacy Survey. The position is at R.A., Dec. = 356.962498, 21.037855 degrees, equivalent to:
   R.A. (J2000) = 23:47:50.99
   Dec. (J2000) = +21:02:16.28
with an uncertainty of 0.5 arcsec.

The following measurements are in the AB magnitude and are not corrected for Galactic extinction:

Mid time   | Exposure Time | Band | Brightness
 263 s     |      50 s  |    VT_B |  21.0+/-0.2  
 263 s     |      50 s  |    VT_R |  18.8+/-0.1 
3.02 h     |  25*100 s  |    VT_B |  23.2+/-0.2
3.02 h     |  27*100 s  |    VT_R |  21.5+/-0.1 

The source showed a slow brightening trend in both channels during our early observations taken within 600 seconds post trigger. It then faded with a temporal decay slope of -1.2 since 1.02 hr. 

Given the fading of the source in our observations, we propose that it is the optical counterpart of the burst.

The Space Variable Objects Monitor (SVOM) is a China-France joint mission led by the Chinese National Space Administration (CNSA, China), National Center for Space Studies (CNES, France) and the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS, China), which is dedicated to observing gamma-ray bursts and other transient phenomena in the energetic universe. VT was jointly developed by Xi'an Institute of Optics and Precision Mechanics (XIOPM), CAS and National astronomical observatories (NAOC), CAS.

We observed the field of GRB 260809A (Brunet et al., GCN45331) using the T193cm telescope at Observatoire de Haute-Provence (France) with the MISTRAL spectro-imager. We obtained 5x600 sec of exposure in the i-band under a ~2arcsec seeing, starting at 20260809_233302UT (~8.6 hr after the Brunet et al. trigger) and ending at 20260810_010555UT (~10.1 hr after the Brunet et al. trigger).
We obtained 3x600 + 4x1000 sec of exposure in the r-band under a ~2arcsec seeing, starting at 20260810_001106UT (~9.2 hr after the Brunet et al. trigger) and ending at 20260810_021627UT (~11.3 hr after the Brunet et al. trigger).
We obtained 900 sec of exposure in the Y-band under a ~2arcsec seeing at 20260809_234858UT (~8.9 hr after the Brunet et al. trigger), 1200 sec at 20260810_023727UT (~11.7 hr after the Brunet et al. trigger), and 1200 sec at 20260810_025838UT (~12 hr after the Brunet et al. trigger).

In the preliminary stacked images, we detect in r and i bands the afterglow at the position of Li et al. (GCN 45336) with preliminary magnitudes of: 

r = 23.36 +/- 0.10 mag
i = 22.03 +/- 0.06 mag

We do not detect the source in our Y-band image. This image has a preliminary estimated limiting magnitude of Y=21.2 (3sigma).

The photometric calibration was performed using nearby stars from the PanSTARRS catalog and the STDWeb/STDPipe tools (Karpov 2025), is in the AB system, and is not corrected for Galactic extinction.

We acknowledge the excellent support from Observatoire de Haute-Provence and in particular Stephane Favard.

EP-FXT performed a follow-up observation of GRB 260809A detected by SVOM/ECLAIRs (Brunet et al., GCN 45331). The observation started at 2026-08-09 17:30:29 (UTC), about 2.55 hr after the SVOM trigger, with a total exposure time of around 3 ks.

GCN Circular 45366: Fermi GBM Sub-Threshold Detection of GRB 260809A

Event ID: GRB 260809A
Submitter: Bagrat Mailyan at Florida Tech <mbagrat@gmail.com>
Created: 2026-08-12 19:55:21 UTC
Source file: 45366.json

B. Mailyan (Florida Tech) reports on behalf of the Fermi-GBM Team:

The SVOM/ECLAIRs detected GRB 260809A on 2026-08-09 at 14:57:33 UTC (Brunet et al. 2026, GCN 45331). There was no Fermi-GBM onboard trigger around this event time. An automated, blind search for gamma-ray bursts below the onboard triggering threshold in Fermi-GBM identified three candidates.

The GBM Targeted Search [1], the most sensitive coherent search for GRB-like signals in GBM, identified a transient starting approximately 12 s after the ECLAIRs best image SNR time of 2026-08-09T14:57:13 UTC. The Targeted Search candidate was found most significantly on the 32 s timescale, with a false alarm rate of 1.6e-04 Hz. The Fermi-MET of the start time of this transient is 807980250.351 s. The Targeted Search localization is found to be spatially consistent with the ECLAIRs localization, with a spatial association probability of 95.8%.

Additionally, the GBM Targeted Search event was found with the highest significance using a "normal" spectrum (Band function with Epeak = 230 keV, alpha = -1.0, beta = -2.3) for a GRB.

The Targeted Search data release product for this event can be found here:
https://fermi.gsfc.nasa.gov/ssc/data/access/gbm/targeted_search/trigger_report.php?trigger_id=807980238&sample=SVOM_GCNs&hash=312d5789ab3c4b28

[1] Goldstein et al. 2019, arXiv:1903.12597
