GCN Circular 45690: GRB 260922A: Swift detection of a burst

Event ID: GRB 260922A
Submitter: K.L. Page at U Leicester <klp5@leicester.ac.uk>
Created: 2026-09-22 10:19:04 UTC
Source file: 45690.json

K. L. Page (U Leicester), P. A. Evans (U Leicester) and
A. Y. Lien (U Tampa) report on behalf of the Neil Gehrels Swift
Observatory Team:

At 10:00:39.44 UT, the Swift Burst Alert Telescope (BAT) triggered and
located GRB 260922A (trigger=1512686).  Swift slewed immediately to the
burst.
The BAT on-board calculated location is
RA, Dec = 227.904, +14.207 deg,  which is
   RA(J2000) = 15h 11m 37s
   Dec(J2000) = 14d 12' 26"
with an uncertainty of 3 arcmin (radius, 90% containment, including
systematic uncertainty).  The BAT light curve showed a multi-peaked
structure with a duration of about 50 sec.  The peak count rate
was ~3000 counts/sec (15-350 keV), at ~1 sec after the trigger.

The XRT began observing the field at 10:02:28.3 UT, 108.9 seconds after
the BAT trigger. Using promptly downlinked data we find a bright,
fading, uncatalogued X-ray source with an enhanced position: RA, Dec
227.90885, 14.21924 which is equivalent to:
   RA(J2000)  = 15h 11m 38.12s
   Dec(J2000) = +14d 13' 09.3"
with an uncertainty of 2.4 arcseconds (radius, 90% containment). This
location is 47 arcseconds from the BAT onboard position, within the BAT
error circle. This position may be improved as more data are received;
the latest position is available at https://www.swift.ac.uk/sper.

A power-law fit to a spectrum formed from promptly downlinked event
data gives a column density in excess of the Galactic value (2.88 x
10^20 cm^-2, Willingale et al. 2013), with an excess column of 5.1
(+2.88/-2.57) x 10^21 cm^-2 (90% confidence).

The initial flux in the 2.5 s image was 2.31e-09 erg cm^-2 s^-1 (0.2-10
keV).

UVOT took a finding chart exposure of 150 seconds with the White filter
starting 122 seconds after the BAT trigger. No credible afterglow
candidate has been found in the initial data products. The 2.7'x2.7'
sub-image covers 100% of the XRT error circle. The typical 3-sigma upper
limit has been about 19.6 mag. The 8'x8' region for the list of sources
generated on-board covers 100% of the XRT error circle. The list of
sources is typically complete to about 18 mag. No correction has been made
for extinction.

Burst Advocate for this burst is K. L. Page (klp5 AT leicester.ac.uk).
Please contact the BA by email if you require additional information
regarding Swift followup of this burst. In extremely urgent cases, after
trying the Burst Advocate, you can contact the Swift PI by phone (see
Swift TOO web site for information: http://www.swift.psu.edu/)

GCN Circular 45697: GRB 260922A: Swift-XRT refined Analysis

Event ID: GRB 260922A
Submitter: Phil Evans at U of Leicester <pae9@star.le.ac.uk>
Created: 2026-09-23 02:13:55 UTC
Source file: 45697.json

V. D'Elia (SSDC & INAF-OAR), D.N. Burrows (PSU), S. Lanava (PSU), S.
Dichiara (PSU), J.P. Osborne (U. Leicester), K.L. Page (U. Leicester),
B. Sbarufatti (INAF-OAB), C. Salvaggio (INAF-OAB) and P.A. Evans report
on behalf of the Swift-XRT team:

We have analysed 6.9 ks of XRT data for GRB 260922A, from 97 s to 33.8
ks after the   trigger. The data comprise 192 s in Windowed Timing (WT)
mode (the first 10 s were taken while Swift was slewing) with the
remainder in Photon Counting (PC) mode. 

The light curve can be modelled with an initial power-law decay with an
index of alpha=2.80 (+0.11, -0.10), followed by a break at T+463 s to
an alpha of 0.77 (+0.06, -0.07).

A spectrum formed from the WT mode data can be fitted with an absorbed
power-law with a photon spectral index	of 2.18 (+0.11, -0.10). The
best-fitting absorption column is  5.1 (+2.0, -1.8) x 10^20 cm^-2, in
excess of the Galactic value of 2.9 x 10^20 cm^-2 (Willingale et al.
2013). The PC mode spectrum has a photon index of 1.70 (+0.15, -0.10)
and a best-fitting absorption column consistent with the Galactic
value. The counts to observed (unabsorbed) 0.3-10 keV flux conversion
factor deduced from this spectrum  is 3.9 x 10^-11 (4.1 x 10^-11) erg
cm^-2 count^-1. 

A summary of the PC-mode spectrum is thus:
Total column:	     2.9 (+3.3, -0.0) x 10^20 cm^-2
Galactic foreground: 2.9 x 10^20 cm^-2
Excess significance: <1.6 sigma
Photon index:	     1.70 (+0.15, -0.10)

If the light curve continues to decay with a power-law decay index of
0.77, the count rate at T+24 hours will be 0.025 count s^-1,
corresponding to an observed (unabsorbed) 0.3-10 keV flux of 9.6 x
10^-13 (1.0 x 10^-12) erg cm^-2 s^-1.

The results of the XRT-team automatic analysis are available at
https://www.swift.ac.uk/xrt_products/01512686.

This circular is an official product of the Swift-XRT team.

The NuSTAR SINGS working group reports the detection of prompt emission from the long-duration GRB 260922A in both the NuSTAR CsI anti-coincidence shields. Details of the search algorithm will be described in a future paper.

The NuSTAR SINGS algorithm, triggered at 2026-09-22T10:00:33.95 UTC, shows a detection of GRB 260922A, also detected by Swift-BAT (GCN 45690) and CALET-GBM (GCN 45716).

The NuSTAR CsI shield data are recorded at 1 Hz. We detect a single peak lasting for about 10-s. The peak count rate is ~1500-cps with a baseline rate of ~1000-cps during this time period. We do not see any evidence in the signal above 100 keV in the CZT detectors.

The Swift-XRT localization (GCN 45696) at RA = 227.90847, Dec = 14.21907 implies an offset from the NuSTAR boresight of 44-deg (i.e. from the side) and an offset from the geocenter of 141-deg.

The time-averaged spectrum of the burst
(measured from T0 to T0+16.640 s)
is best fit in the 20 keV - 10 MeV range
by a power law with exponential cutoff model:
dN/dE ~ (E^alpha)*exp(-E*(2+alpha)/Ep)
with alpha = -0.31(-0.19,+0.21)
and Ep = 151(-9,+10) keV (chi2 = 101/98 dof).

and an upper limit on the high energy photon index: beta < -2.6
(chi2 = 101/97 dof).

The spectrum near the maximum count rate
(measured from T0+0.256 to T0+8.448 s)

with alpha = -0.39(-0.15,+0.16)

C.C. Cheung, R. Woolf, M. Kerr, J.E. Grove (NRL), A. Goldstein (USRA), C.A. Wilson-Hodge, D. Kocevski (MSFC), and M.S. Briggs (UAH) report:

The Glowbug gamma-ray telescope [1,2,3], operating on the International Space Station, reports the detection of GRB 260922A, which was also detected by Swift/BAT (GCN 45690, 45703), CALET (GCN 45716), NuSTAR (GCN 45728), and Konus-Wind (GCN 45729)

Using an adaptive window with a resolution of 32-ms, the burst onset is determined to be 2026-09-22 10:00:37.128 with a duration of 2.8 s and a total significance of about 14.9 sigma.  The light curve comprises a single peak.

The analysis results presented here are preliminary and use a response function that lacks a detailed characterization of the surrounding passive structure of the ISS. Glowbug is a NASA-funded technology demonstrator for sensitive, low-cost gamma-ray transient telescopes developed, built, and operated by the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory (NRL) with support from the University of Alabama in Huntsville, USRA, and NASA MSFC.
